# Impact-to-funding skill — first-release scope and method

**Date:** 2026-11-04. **Status:** proposed product/method scope, prepared within the selected scoping task. Implementation, public repository creation, publication, outreach, integrations and money movement are not selected by this document.

“Impact-to-funding” is a working description, not a selected product name. This workspace planning file is separate from private project evidence. Its eventual repository, licence and publication contents remain to be decided.

## 1. Outcome and audience

Help individuals explain a clear theory of change, substantiate specific claims about their contribution, and prepare an honest, useful case for funding. Preserve a project-assessment mode; individuals may contribute to several projects and projects may have many contributors.

The intended long-term sequence is:

> Clearer reasoning and proportionate evidence → credible, understandable profiles and reports → suitable funders can make better-informed decisions → useful work receives support → that work may produce social benefit.

These are hypotheses about this product, not demonstrated effects. More polished reports, donations received, grant allocation, and social benefit are different outcomes. The skill must be able to help someone conclude that a project should change, support an existing effort, or stop—not merely make every project sound fundable.

### Requirements supplied by Artem

- Alignment with Moral Ambition/Bregman's thinking, without claiming an official methodology or endorsement.
- Theory-of-change development and assessment of specific projects, with a path for individuals to fundraise using clear theories and verified impact claims.
- An open-source skill; an MCP server only if justified.
- A SENRI assessment pilot, followed by publication and a separately approved introduction to the Moral Ambition community.
- Future modalities: a website widget, Open Collective, Ko-fi, and machine-readable reporting that could support grant distribution among many builders.

### Proposed first-release boundary

A reusable conversational skill that produces portable, human-readable Markdown. It works with material the person supplies or explicitly authorises it to inspect. It does not require a hosted account, public registry, payment account, new database, widget, formal manifest standard, or MCP server.

The first usable result is a **funding-ready draft**, not automatic certification: a person can review the profile, see what is supported or missing, and reuse approved text with their existing fundraising destination. Publication and sending an ask remain separate actions.

People without demonstrated outcomes can still produce an explicitly prospective funding proposal. They must not be pressured to manufacture retrospective impact to qualify. Conversely, historical results cannot automatically justify claims about what the next grant will achieve.

## 2. First-release outputs

| Output | Minimum useful content |
|---|---|
| Impact profile | Person or chosen public identity; role and relevant projects; intended beneficiaries; purpose; actual contribution; links to the other outputs; dates and evidence limitations. No moral-worth ranking. |
| Theory of change | Problem, affected people, importance, specific neglected gap, plausible fixability, activities, outputs, outcomes, intended impact, assumptions, alternatives, dependencies, harms and scope of responsibility. |
| Evidence and assumptions register | Individual claims linked to evidence, observations separated from projections, alternative explanations, uncertainty, contradictions, missing perspectives, and private/public disclosure limits. |
| Proportionate evaluation plan | A decision to inform, feasible evidence collection, baseline/comparison where appropriate, qualitative perspectives, burden/consent, review timing and stopping conditions. |
| Funding brief | What support enables, amount/currency/period if known, actual cost basis, milestones or learning goals, dependencies, existing relevant support, risks and a person-supplied funding destination if available. Unknowns remain unknown; no invented budgets or promised returns. |
| Periodic report | Reporting period, previous intentions, what happened and did not, evidence, others' contributions, expenditure information where available and appropriate, harms, changed beliefs, funding needs and next decisions. |

Outputs can be incomplete or combined when that is clearer. A one-page project should not require six lengthy documents. A public-ready candidate must be a separately curated derivative, never a direct dump of a private evidence register.

## 3. Guided method

### Step 1 — locate the real task and project stage

Accept a description, existing documents, an existing theory, or new evidence. Identify the current decision and whether the work is discovery, planned intervention, ongoing delivery, retrospective review, or a portfolio of contributions. Ask only for information that materially changes the current output; use existing authorised records before repeating questions.

Establish whether the subject is an individual contribution or a project's outcomes. Do not silently convert someone else's work into the individual's impact. Do not imply a person speaks for a community, partner or organisation without a basis.

### Step 2 — examine purpose and alternatives

Who benefits, who bears costs, and who helps define success? Apply importance, neglectedness and fixability to the particular problem and intervention, not broad cause labels. Consider supporting existing work, simpler methods, doing nothing new and opportunity costs. Keep qualitative benefits and long-term/systemic change visible even when they resist numerical comparison.

### Step 3 — construct and challenge the causal pathway

Separate activities → outputs → outcomes → intended impact. Ask why each link should hold, what could break it, who controls it, and what would likely happen without this contribution. Preserve uncertain causal links rather than making the prose sound settled. Invite affected people's interpretation where feasible and consented; the AI cannot invent their approval or substitute a simulated stakeholder for an actual voice.

### Step 4 — assess claims and plan learning

Use the claim/review rules below. Choose the smallest sufficient assessment: existing records, short qualitative review, baseline/follow-up, a safe functional check, or another justified method. Match effort to consequence and available capacity. Strong causal attribution may require evidence outside this skill's capabilities; state the limitation rather than imply that a confident assessment establishes causality.

Document what evidence would change the conclusion. The current output may be a learning plan rather than an impact finding. Do not introduce a survey, experiment, dashboard or reusable evaluation runner solely to make the assessment look rigorous.

### Step 5 — prepare the funding case without upgrading claims

Describe the proposed marginal use of funds, delivery/learning responsibility, costs, risks, and how progress will be reported. Keep aspiration, observed outcome, plausible contribution and independently evaluated causal finding distinct. Report shared contributions; do not add jointly produced outcomes across people/projects as if each independently caused them.

The skill can examine whether a funding destination is consistent with supplied evidence, but does not establish legal eligibility, identity, account ownership, tax deductibility or receipt of funds merely from a URL. Never infer that support for a project authorises fundraising in another person's name.

### Step 6 — review disclosure and update over time

Keep a private working version distinct from any public candidate. Ask what can be shared, with whom, and whether necessary consent exists. Show material limitations beside claims, not only in a distant disclaimer. Preserve corrections and the supersession of older claims; an old report is not current progress.

At a later review, compare what was expected with what happened, include abandoned actions and failed hypotheses, and revise the theory or ask. Do not make reporting frequency or confidence growth a success metric by default.

## 4. What “verified” means

**Verification is claim-specific, evidence-specific, scoped and dated.** The first release does not operate an accreditation body or provide a blanket “verified impact” badge. It can record and explain an actual review by a person or organisation; it cannot create their attestation or independence.

Use separate dimensions, not a ladder that implies stronger evidence simply because someone else read it:

| Dimension | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| Claim type | Activity/output, observed outcome, causal/contribution claim, forecast/proposed outcome, assumption, or financial statement. |
| Evidence assessment | Supported within stated scope, partly supported, insufficient, conflicting, or not assessed; include reasoning and known limitations. These are proposed descriptive labels, not a validated scoring scale. |
| Review provenance | Self-report; AI-assisted examination; named peer/partner review; or documented independent evaluation. Record the real reviewer, role, date, scope, method, interests and reference when known. Unknown independence stays unknown. |
| Currency and disclosure | Reporting period, observation/source dates, review date, appropriate recheck conditions, and which material may be public. Access restrictions and unavailable evidence remain visible. |

A review event refers to a specific claim and version of the underlying material. A later edit or different claim does not silently inherit the earlier review. Formal signing, public identity verification and machine-readable attestation formats are later design questions, not first-release requirements.

### Minimum conceptual claim record

Claim statement and subject; person/project role; affected group and scope; time period; claim type; evidence references and their dates; known provenance/limitations; assessment and rationale; reviewer information if an actual review exists; other contributors and plausible alternatives; disclosure permission; and correction/dispute state if relevant.

This is a content contract, not a published schema or a claim of compatibility with an existing `funding.json` format.

### Examples of boundaries

- A receipt may support expenditure, not the claimed social benefit of that expenditure.
- Attendance supports participation volume, not improved decisions or preparedness.
- A partner can attest to an observed result without proving the project's causal effect or being independent.
- AI checking that cited material supports the wording is not independent impact verification or proof that the underlying material is authentic.
- Money received is a fundraising result, not a unit of social impact. Repeat donations do not establish effectiveness.
- A plausible forecast can justify learning or preparedness, but is not an observed outcome or an estimate of lives saved.

Material contradictory evidence, unresolved disputes and conflicts of interest must not be hidden to strengthen a funding case. Public disclosure of a dispute must itself respect privacy, fairness and consent; a private allegation is not automatically publishable fact.

## 5. Privacy, agency and equitable access

- No automatic publishing, contacting reviewers/funders, account creation, evidence uploads to external services, fund applications or payments. Use existing tool permissions and obtain the applicable authorisation for external actions.
- Collect only necessary information. No default demand for a legal identity, exact location, beneficiary identities, medical information or donor list. Future funders/payment providers may have requirements; these are not invented as universal skill requirements.
- Do not treat third-party permission to record as blanket permission to publish or reuse a story in fundraising. Agree access, purpose and retention appropriate to the data.
- Accept oral accounts transcribed with permission, locally held evidence and qualitative accounts where appropriate, with explicit limits. Do not claim audio/image/video ingestion works unless the actual host tools support it.
- Allow a credible learning proposal and an honest account of failure; avoid privileging polished English, large reporting budgets or high-frequency public reporting as evidence of impact.
- Do not score people against each other, make universal moral comparisons, infer undisclosed personal traits, or prescribe a person's career as morally mandatory.
- The skill should explain which judgement belongs to the person, affected people, a qualified evaluator or a funder. It assists those judgements; it does not replace them.

## 6. Source and rights boundaries

Primary framing sources checked on 2026-11-04:

1. [School for Moral Ambition — principles](https://www.moralambition.org/principles): prioritisation of big, neglected, fixable problems; warning against measurability bias; robust theories of change; epistemic humility, compassion and sustainable ambition.
2. [School for Moral Ambition — Circles](https://www.moralambition.org/circles): its public description includes stakeholder mapping, strengths and a personal theory of change. This does not establish a standard impact score or a ready-made verification protocol.

The claim records, funding workflow, review model and evaluation design in this document are **our proposed implementation choices**, not attributed to Bregman or the School as their methodology. Public descriptions do not validate this method's effectiveness.

Use original wording, links and limited attributed paraphrases. Do not reproduce book text, private Circle materials or exercises merely because they are accessible. A public URL is not an open licence. Any later source-derived template needs a recorded reuse basis before inclusion. Do not use School/Bregman branding or suggest affiliation. Verify code/content/dependency licence compatibility before release; the licence choice remains open.

Private pilot evidence is not open-source source material. SENRI documents stay in their private repository. The reusable skill must not contain participant details, private excerpts, contact information, sensitive strategy, or a disguised version of those facts. A synthetic example must be genuinely synthetic; public project examples require deliberate clearance.

## 7. Later modalities — independent scopes

| Capability | Intended role | Boundary and dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Website widget | Embed a reviewed public profile, report/evidence summaries and funding links. | Requires agreed public content, accessible presentation, control over updates/corrections and hosting; not a first-release requirement. No implication that a badge or embed guarantees effectiveness. |
| Open Collective | Connect a funding case with an appropriate funding destination and, where supported and authorised, financial information. | Supported capabilities, ownership, access and privacy must be established before integration. No API, eligibility or tax claim is assumed here. |
| Ko-fi | Provide a contribution route or later authorised connection. | Begin from a person-supplied link if available; deeper capabilities and ownership remain to be checked. No live writes or payment handling selected. |
| Funding/report manifest | Enable discovery and reuse of profiles, funding needs, review provenance and periodic reports. | Identify Artem's intended `funding.json` reference before choosing compatibility, extension or a new format. A public manifest must not expose private evidence or imply verified ownership. |
| Report-based grant distribution | Potentially allocate support across many builders using transparent reporting and explicit fund policy. | Separate system requiring eligibility, identity/fraud/Sybil controls, reporting-burden fairness, duplicate/shared-contribution handling, conflicts, published allocation rules, challenge/appeal process, payment constraints and explicit disbursement authority. Clear evidence is not itself an allocation formula. |
| MCP interface | Make a demonstrated workflow capability available to compatible clients. | Only when an actual integration gap justifies it. Does not replace hosting, evidence governance or payment services. |

No specific platform contract, existing manifest, funding algorithm, blockchain, wallet, token or distribution model has been selected or verified. A thousand-builder allocation pilot is not a sensible prerequisite for validating a conversational skill.

## 8. First-release acceptance and validation

The proposed release should pass these cases using existing host tools and simple fixtures rather than requiring bespoke infrastructure:

1. **SENRI, private project case:** handle discovery versus a conditional intervention, stale/differently dated evidence, unclear roles, missing local agreement and protected source material without inventing commitments or publishing it.
2. **Individual-builder case:** connect the person's real or synthetic role to shared project outcomes; distinguish verified evidence from an unreviewed claim; produce a realistic, explicitly conditional funding ask without claiming all project impact personally.
3. **Contrasting synthetic case:** handle no demonstrated outcomes, contradictory evidence, qualitative benefit, absent stakeholders or an unjustified funding claim. Narrow or reject the claim and offer a proportionate learning plan instead of laundering it into a persuasive story.

Across cases, acceptance requires traceable claims; no invented evidence, reviewer or budget; visible uncertainty and failure; proportionate questioning; clear disclosure boundaries; and a useful next decision. The profile and funding brief should be understandable without reading the full evidence register. A complete draft can pass without independent verification if it honestly says what remains unverified; it must not be presented as a verified-impact profile in that state.

Local checks and AI review establish neither human usefulness nor causal effectiveness. A person should review the pilot outputs and task-owned changes before release. Implementation and such validation remain separate roadmap work, not completed by writing this scope.

## 9. Product learning and delivery boundaries

Early learning should ask whether people clarify their intended contribution, avoid unsupported claims, produce a reusable funding case with tolerable effort, and find it useful in actual funder conversations. Voluntarily track fundraising results with context, missingness and other influences; do not claim the skill caused a donation from before/after anecdotes alone. Later assess whether supported work achieves its intended outcomes, without making reporting burden greater than its decision value.

The dependency path is scope/method → private pilot review and reusable skill implementation → validation/revision → release preparation → approved publication and install verification → approved community introduction and ongoing learning. Integrations, manifest design, automatic distribution and a SENRI field intervention are independent conditional branches, not release gates. Existing completed draft work need not be repeated.

Before implementation, choose the skill's repository/location and include preservation of this planning draft in that decision. Before publication, confirm public contents, rights/licences, ownership and distribution channel. Before sharing with the Moral Ambition community, approve the exact message and destination separately. No draft or menu selection substitutes for these actual external-action boundaries.
