# Builder check-ins and peer feedback: assessment

Assessment for [#36](https://github.com/zhiganov/impact-to-funding/issues/36), 2026-11-11. It asks whether a short, voluntary check-in with optional feedback from a peer would help ItF users more than what already exists. All examples are fictional. No check-ins were run with people.

Pioneer's [retrospective](https://pioneer.app/blog/pioneer/) inspired the question. Its reported activity doesn't show that check-ins would help ItF users.

## What already exists

| Existing guidance | What it covers | What it doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| [Periodic report](../assets/report.md) | A full account for a period: intentions, what happened, evidence, costs, harms, changes and disclosure | It's long for a quick "where am I?" moment, and it isn't built around asking someone for help |
| [Contribution guidance](../references/contributions.md) | The person's role, shared work, capacity and portfolio | No regular rhythm or feedback step |
| [#16 collaboration design](https://github.com/zhiganov/impact-to-funding/issues/16#issuecomment-6665079951) | Shared theories, learning reports, decisions and dissent within a project team | Aimed at a team's shared record, not one person asking a peer for a view |

**The gap:** a short, private moment to take stock and ask one person for one specific piece of feedback. That's lighter than a report, and it's for one person rather than a team.

## A minimal check-in

Six questions, answered in a few lines each. Any can be skipped.

1. **What I meant to do** since last time.
2. **What happened,** including what didn't, maintenance and failed attempts.
3. **What I'm unsure of,** and what evidence I have or lack.
4. **What I learned,** or which assumption changed.
5. **The next decision** I face, if there is one.
6. **One request for help,** if any: a specific question for a specific person.

It doesn't need a public theory of change, a startup, a fundraising goal or numbers. Hard-to-measure work counts.

## Audience, timing and feedback

- **Private by default.** The person chooses whether anyone sees it, and who.
- **Their timing.** There's no required cadence: weekly, after a milestone, or only when stuck.
- **A bounded feedback request.** The person asks one named peer one question, through a channel they already use. The peer can accept or decline, without giving a reason. Nobody is assigned as a reviewer.
- **Only what they choose to share.** Project evidence isn't sent by default. Sharing a check-in doesn't grant permission to reuse it.

## Formative feedback isn't review or funding

- **It isn't claim review** under [#15](https://github.com/zhiganov/impact-to-funding/issues/15). A peer's comment on a check-in doesn't support or challenge a specific claim unless that claim and version were actually examined.
- **It isn't evaluation, endorsement or certification.**
- **It doesn't affect funding decisions.**
- **The builder decides.** Disagreement is fine and stays on record if they want.
- **A mentor isn't required.** The [mentor pool scoping](mentor-pool-scoping.md) is separate; either can exist without the other.

## Fictional examples

- **A useful response.** Kai writes a check-in about a city-data tool and asks a former colleague: "Is the import step worth automating, or is it rare?" The colleague says it's rare. Kai drops the automation and spends the time on documentation.
- **A changed plan.** Rosa's check-in notes that nobody used the new map feature in a month. Writing it down, she decides to pause the feature and ask two users why, before building more.
- **Missing evidence.** Jun writes "workshops are helping people find jobs" under "what happened". Rewriting it under "what I'm unsure of", he notes he has attendance numbers but no follow-up. He changes the claim to "people attend; effects unknown".
- **Declined feedback.** Mo asks a peer to read a check-in. The peer has no time and declines. Mo isn't told why and doesn't treat it as a verdict on the work.
- **Private material.** Ana's check-in mentions a partner's internal report. She keeps the check-in private, and when asking for feedback describes the situation without sharing the report.

Usefulness here means a clearer decision, something learned or practical help. It doesn't mean more updates, votes, output or donations. These examples are authored, not observed.

## Recommendation

**Documentation only.** Keep the six-question check-in in this note as an optional prompt that people can use with any agent or by hand. The skill already supports it: someone can ask ItF to help them take stock, and the existing guidance applies. No new template, cadence, tool or community is needed.

**Consider a bounded trial later** only with a willing host, a few consenting participants, an existing channel, a clear effort limit and agreed handling of what's shared.

## Limits

This is desk assessment only. No one was asked to do check-ins or give feedback. It doesn't need Groups or native installation.
