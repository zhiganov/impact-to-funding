# A case-grounded pattern language with framework lenses

**Design proposal, 2026-11-08.** This describes a possible reference library for
ItF, not an implemented collection, validated pattern language, database, search
service or executable schema. The worked example is entirely fictional. No broad
collection, automatic extraction, outreach, paid evaluation or installed-skill
change follows from this design.

The subsequently prepared [seed reference collection](../references/library/README.md)
is a separate, bounded curation stage. It does not turn this document's fictional
worked example into real evidence or establish pattern validity, exhaustive
coverage, database implementation or automated skill routing.

A later [core-skill integration](../SKILL.md#use-reference-knowledge-selectively)
adds optional, selective reading of those notes through the host's existing tools.
That is not a database, collector, automatic recommendation engine or a change
to the systems/complexity method proposed as a separate scope.

## Purpose

Help people recognise possibilities for contributing, examine their local fit
and learn from others without copying projects or treating persuasive stories as
proof of impact. Serve the [agreed purpose](audience-and-positioning.md), not a
mandatory method or assessment funnel.

Combine **experience-grounded patterns** with **framework-informed questions**.
Cases can challenge patterns; frameworks can expose omissions; local experience
can suggest revisions to either. Frameworks do not supply evidence that a pattern
worked, and repeated cases do not settle whose values should shape the work.

Climate, food systems and civic tech are starting examples, not domain boundaries.
Health, education, housing, care, biodiversity and other domains may be relevant.
The earlier six-case starter idea is a possible bounded collection slice, not a
domain cap, coverage claim or requirement to find six success stories. This
library is optional enrichment, not a prerequisite for using or learning about ItF.

## Four linked kinds of knowledge

| Kind | What it explains | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Case | A particular context, actual/proposed work, documented theory, results and uncertainty. | That the work succeeded, caused all observed change or transfers elsewhere. |
| Pattern | A recurring problem and set of tensions, with an adaptable response and applicability limits. | A universal recipe or impact certificate. |
| Framework lens | A perspective, its useful questions, assumptions, scope and limitations. | Local consent, observed outcomes or compatibility with every other lens. |
| Local application | How a person/project interprets relevant references in its own circumstances. | An approved shared theory, committed role or right to publish the underlying material. |

Public reference records stay separate from private personal/project theories,
evidence, relationships and applications. A local application can reference exact
public versions without exporting its own evidence. Personal and shared project
theories remain distinct under the [contribution guidance](../references/contributions.md).
No link automatically transfers evidence, reviews, permission or authority.

## Minimal record content

These are reading/authoring conventions, not compulsory forms or a data standard.
Keep short notes short; retain explicit unknowns instead of inventing completeness.
Public records need a stable identifier, title, revision and a distinction between
their sources and the record author's interpretation.

### Case

- **Context:** domain(s), scale/period where relevant and safe, problem, affected
  people, existing provision and other contributors.
- **Theory provenance:** a published theory and its author/edition, or an explicitly
  labelled reconstruction. Do not imply a retrospective reconstruction guided the
  original work.
- **Pathway:** actual/proposed activities, mechanism, intended changes, assumptions,
  dependencies and material alternatives.
- **Results:** observations versus forecasts, failures, adaptations, harms and
  missing perspectives. Link evaluations separately from the project's own account;
  record what was inspected and the actual basis for reviewer independence.
- **Sources and reuse:** source author/owner, source version/date and retrieval
  date, inspected versus merely linked material, access and reuse basis.
- **Interpretation:** possible pattern connections and transfer questions, attributed
  to the case-note author rather than silently assigned to the original project.

Assess individual claims under [claim review](../references/claims.md), not a
case-wide success score. Funding, reach, reputation, a clear theory or an
evaluation's existence do not establish effectiveness. Include mixed and negative
results rather than selecting only prominent or surviving projects.

### Pattern

- **Context and problem:** when the problem recurs and who experiences it.
- **Forces:** competing needs, incentives, power and constraints that make a
  response difficult, not just a list of desirable values.
- **Response:** a useful arrangement, relationship or practice people can adapt;
  explain the conditions its mechanism needs rather than prescribing fixed steps.
- **Consequences and limits:** trade-offs, exclusion risks, failure conditions,
  alternatives and contexts where the pattern should not be used.
- **Basis:** supporting and challenging case/claim versions, or a clearly marked
  theoretical/practitioner proposal. Separate case-derived, framework-informed and
  mixed origins. Framework inspiration is not empirical support.
- **Connections:** related patterns, variants, tensions and relevant lenses, with
  an explanation of each relationship.

One case can motivate a candidate but cannot establish recurrence or broad
transferability. Distinguish proposed analogy from comparison supported by
independent cases. Record actual practitioner review and disagreements when
available; do not invent consensus or treat AI synthesis as practitioner validation.
No universal confidence number, maturity badge or staffed review service is assumed.

### Framework lens

Record author/source, edition where known, purpose, useful questions, scope,
limitations and reuse terms. Distinguish original ItF questions from the author's
published method. Normative, explanatory and methodological lenses are not
interchangeable checklists:

- **Doughnut Economics:** consider social needs, ecological effects, distribution,
  power and whether growth is desirable. Retain both social foundation and
  ecological ceiling; do not reduce the framework to an alignment tag.
- **Planetary Boundaries:** examine environmental dependencies and burden shifting.
  Global boundaries are not automatic local quotas or personal scores; numerical
  translation needs a justified method and explicit assumptions.
- **Systems/complexity perspectives:** examine relationships, uncertainty and the
  limits of a pathway. Any future Cynefin reference must retain its own source
  meaning, not become an "advanced" score or be silently blended with another method.

The existing [framework starter](../references/frameworks.md) supplies dated
Planetary Boundaries and Doughnut notes, not an exhaustive library. This design
does not change the skill's systems/complexity instructions or implement Cynefin;
that remains a separate method-design scope. People may decline a lens, use an
appropriate local framework or leave a question unresolved.

## Relationships make it a language

Links identify source and target revisions, relationship, reasoning, scope and
author. They are authored interpretations unless explicitly attributed otherwise.

| Relationship | Meaning and limit |
|---|---|
| Case supports/challenges a pattern | A particular observation bears on a particular part of the pattern, not blanket endorsement. |
| Pattern complements another | Responses may reinforce each other in the stated context, not a mandatory bundle. |
| Pattern is a variant of another | A related idea has materially different conditions or implementation; explain the difference. |
| Patterns are in tension | Addressing one problem can worsen another; preserve the trade-off. |
| Lens questions a case or pattern | It exposes a question, assumption or omission, not proof of violation or conformance. |
| Local application adapts a pattern | A provisional use elsewhere, not evidence the source's results will recur. |

Do not infer links transitively: sharing a case or lens does not make patterns
corroborate each other. Record a prerequisite only when independently justified;
reading order is not a dependency. The language should help people compose and
adapt responses, not require completion of every connected pattern.

Use multiple open-ended discovery facets: domain, affected need, recurring
problem, proposed mechanism, scale, constraints and lens. Domains can overlap and
new terms can be added. Shared keywords are not evidence of a shared mechanism.
Distinguish domain-specific patterns from cross-domain candidates and explain
the proposed basis for transfer.

## From references to a local theory of change

This is a possible reading practice, not a new mandatory skill workflow:

1. Start from the person's situation or work, not a framework menu. Relevant known
   material or no external reference may be sufficient.
2. Read a small relevant selection with its context and limitations. A summary
   does not replace inspecting underlying evidence for consequential claims.
3. Use a lens only where it reveals something material. Preserve disagreements;
   do not average lenses into a score or imply their labels implement their methods.
4. Explain what differs locally: affected people's perspectives, roles, authority,
   incentives, resources, scale and relationships. Domain similarity is not enough.
5. If useful, propose an adaptation and provisional explanation of how it might
   help. Separate expectations from evidence; identify what could undermine it.
   Retain alternative pathways and learning questions in uncertain settings rather
   than manufacturing a guaranteed causal chain.
6. Keep the application in the person's chosen private conversation or location.
   A possible role is not a commitment; a draft project theory is not collectively
   adopted; a reference does not authorise contact or new evidence collection.

Permission for a private application does not permit adding it to the library.
A later public case needs its own sources, disclosure review and reuse basis.
Do not automatically ingest user conversations, project histories, affiliations,
beneficiary accounts or reviews to enrich the collection.

## Worked example — entirely fictional

These labels illustrate relationships, not populated catalogue records. There
are no real participants, observed results or reviewed patterns in this example.

- **X-C1, civic software:** a fictional group builds a useful-looking service, but
  continuing issue handling has no agreed owner or resources. Demand and public
  benefit remain unestablished.
- **X-C2, community food provision:** a fictional shared facility needs recurring
  safety checks and coordination. Its launch plan names equipment but no accepted
  continuing responsibilities. No actual safety, waste or access outcome is claimed.

**X-P1, candidate: provide for ongoing stewardship before expansion.** Context:
people may depend on work requiring recurring care. Forces: visible new delivery
attracts attention while upkeep costs persist; assigning responsibility can create
unpaid burden or exclude people without spare capacity. Proposed response: make
continuing tasks visible and establish willing responsibility, resources, handover
and an exit/review route before treating expansion as sustainable. Limits: do not
preserve harmful or unwanted work, assume unpaid capacity or turn stewardship into
an unaccountable gatekeeper role.

X-C1 and X-C2 motivate a **proposed cross-domain analogy**, not empirical support
for X-P1. Real independent references and counterexamples would be needed to
examine it. A climate-adaptation application is another possible comparison to
research, not a third observed success. Other patterns might complement or
conflict with X-P1; none are invented as established entries here.

**Original ItF lens questions:** a Doughnut-informed inquiry could ask whose
needs are met, who bears upkeep costs, who has a voice and what ecological burdens
arise. A Planetary-Boundaries-informed inquiry could ask which environmental
pressures matter and whether burdens shift elsewhere. These are limited questions,
not copied worksheets, quantitative allocations or full framework assessments.

**Private local application:** a contributor could explore a bounded stewardship
role with actual capacity and support needs explicit. A provisional theory might
be that appropriately resourced upkeep makes continued use more plausible,
subject to real need, agreed responsibility and observed experience. It must not
claim upkeep already delivered access, waste reduction or climate benefit. The
library offers a possibility, not an assignment, promised funding or a requirement
to produce a theory before contributing.

## Storage and future use

If collection is selected, start with ordinary versioned text records and a small
linked index. Separate case, pattern and lens notes can use stable identifiers;
this design prescribes no paths, root manifest, JSON dialect or hosted database.
Keep source dates, record revisions and actual review versions distinct. Edits do
not inherit old reviews or rewrite historical evidence.

A future skill link could load only relevant notes, with sources, interpretation
and limitations visible. The library would be optional, not an always-loaded
prompt, live collector or automatic recommendation engine. No routing/retrieval
feature is implemented by this design record itself; the later core-skill link
implements conditional reference reading, not a new retrieval service.

Consider a database or separate distribution project when actual concurrent
editing, retrieval, updating or multiple consumers justify it, with ownership,
access, costs and maintenance established. File count alone does not decide the
architecture; no staffed operator is assumed. Future source/rights review can use
an appropriate existing repository workflow. Record who actually reviewed what;
maintainer acceptance is not independent impact verification.

Prefer original source-linked summaries; public access is not permission to bundle
books, paid material, diagrams, worksheets, stories or datasets. Check applicable
rights before importing/adapting them. Names, branding and source licences remain
separate from ItF's [own licences](../LICENSE). Preserve applicable share-alike obligations for
actual adaptations; DEAL asks users not to generate Doughnut diagrams with AI.
Follow [sources and rights](../SOURCES.md) and ordinary privacy/disclosure rules.

**Established by this design:** a proposed knowledge structure, contextual links,
transfer limits, open domains, fictional walkthrough and storage recommendation.
**Not established by this design alone:** real case coverage, recurring mechanisms, pattern validity,
user usefulness, implemented catalogue/routing, operational capacity or changes to
the skill's systems/complexity method. Seed curation, implementation, publication
and any installed-version update retain their scope and approval boundaries.
