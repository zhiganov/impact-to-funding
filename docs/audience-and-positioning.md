# Audience and positioning: working hypotheses

**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Status:** agent-native builders are the agreed initial primary audience for positioning. Employment and income insecurity are one important job to be done, not a condition shared by everyone in that audience. The broader intention is to channel people's energy, skills, experience and wisdom towards real problems and verifiable contributions, with like-minded peers, communities, funders and potential financial support. ItF's usefulness and these intended outcomes remain unvalidated. This note does not select recruitment, connection services or changes to the published product positioning or skill workflow.

## Initial primary audience: agent-native builders

Developers, open-source maintainers and technically fluent independent builders already working with coding agents are the initial primary audience for a product delivered as an agent skill. Researchers and other practitioners with established agent-skill workflows may also fit; professional title matters less than the existing practice.

The user is not simply anyone who could benefit from impact planning. It is someone already using an agent to do real work who wants to put their capacity and judgement towards problems that matter, potentially while losing paid work or struggling to get hired. Others may be securely employed, maintaining an existing project or exploring a different direction. They need not already identify as a social-impact practitioner, want to become a founder or lead a nonprofit. A broad expression of the job is:

> Help me put my skills, energy and experience towards a real problem, work with people who care about it, establish what difference we make, and find support to keep contributing.

For someone facing displacement, greater ability to produce with an agent does not compensate for losing employment, income or bargaining power. Independent work may be a constrained response to employment insecurity, not a freely chosen entrepreneurial opportunity. Preserve that economic context without making unemployment the defining trait of the whole audience.

This route does not require introducing both an unfamiliar agent workflow and an impact-planning method at once. That is an adoption hypothesis, not measured evidence of daily skill usage, demand for ItF or willingness to seek funding. Neither code output nor agent productivity establishes social benefit.

The person chooses the purpose and authorises actions; the agent reads and applies the skill. Direct execution by an agent and the human audience's need are different layers. Installing the skill does not authorise publishing, outreach or fundraising.

For initial audience prioritisation, distinguish broad potential usefulness from an existing practice through which people can actually adopt the skill. Consider the overlap of habitual agent use and these contribution, collaboration, evidence and support needs, rather than inferring the primary audience from either social purpose alone or tool familiarity alone. Do not infer that every member has the same employment situation or motivation. The broader roles below remain useful, but they do not establish that initial audience.

## Jobs to be done and intended outcomes

| Situation or need | Intended help |
|---|---|
| Job loss, difficulty getting hired or insecure income | Explore a chosen route to supported useful contributions without promising replacement employment or requiring entrepreneurship. |
| Energy and experience without a clear worthwhile direction | Identify a real, community-defined problem and a proportionate contribution, including supporting existing work rather than creating another project. |
| Wanting to contribute with others rather than alone | Find and develop relationships with like-minded peers and relevant communities; clarify shared purpose, distinct roles and actual capacity. |
| Wanting to know whether the work really helps | Develop a theory of change, examine evidence, learn from results and make bounded claims that can be checked or reviewed. |
| Needing resources to continue useful work | Explain what support enables, connect with appropriate funders or other supporters where authorised, and pursue a sustainable contribution path. |

These are related but distinct jobs. A person may arrive through one or several of them. Money matters, especially when income is insecure; peer relationships, belonging, shared learning and collaboration are also intended benefits, not merely steps in a fundraising funnel.

The vision includes connections with peers, communities and funders, not just better private documents. It does not establish that the current skill supplies a network, matching service, introductions or funding. Existing communities and human-facilitated connections may serve the need; no new platform is presumed necessary. Any outreach, profile publication or introduction still needs the appropriate consent and approval.

## Verified impact as an ambition, not an automatic status

The goal is solving real problems with impact that can be substantiated and, where appropriate, independently checked. Evidence and review should strengthen actual contribution and learning rather than merely make a funding story sound convincing.

Use dated, scoped claims; preserve uncertainty, other contributors, counterevidence and affected people's perspectives. Record what was actually reviewed, by whom and against what evidence. A theory of change, completed report, signature, peer connection or funding receipt is not itself verified impact. Prospective work can be worth supporting before outcomes exist, without labelling its expected effects verified. This ambition does not introduce a blanket certification scheme or require a new assessment engine.

## Broader relevant audiences

People leading small, socially useful initiatives may benefit from ItF when they need to understand and explain their contribution without a dedicated evaluation or fundraising team. Examples include independent contributors, community organisers, public-interest or open-source builders, and small nonprofit or social-enterprise teams.

The common situation is more useful than an organisation label:

> I am doing work I believe matters. What difference could it realistically make, what can I substantiate, and what support would help?

This describes a plausible user need, not the complete audience or the primary segment by itself. ItF can also support an individual's direction or bounded contributions across shared projects; the person does not have to lead a project or supply a personal theory of change for project-only work.

## Needs across the work's lifecycle

| Situation | Potential value |
|---|---|
| Before starting | Decide whether to act, support an existing effort, change direction or stop. |
| While working | Connect activities to intended outcomes, examine assumptions and choose proportionate ways to learn. |
| Seeking support | Explain what additional resources enable without exaggerating past contribution or presenting forecasts as results. |
| Reporting or reviewing | Distinguish what happened, the person's actual contribution, remaining uncertainty, changed plans and abandoned or unsuccessful work. |

Funding is an important use case, not an entry requirement. A clearer decision, a narrower claim, a useful connection, support for existing work or a decision to stop can be a useful result even when no money is raised. Do not imply that these benefits compensate for unmet financial needs. Project theories and optional personal theories remain separate.

## Other relevant roles

- **Facilitators, advisers and community-support organisations** may use the skill to help people reason through their work. Assistance does not give them authority to speak for a project or affected people.
- **Funders and donors** may read resulting briefs and reports or use the method to ask better questions. Whether they are direct users, readers or another audience is not settled here. The current skill is not a grant-selection system, comparative impact-ranking service or certification scheme.
- **Affected people and communities** are not merely readers of a report. Their definitions of success and actual perspectives should inform the work. Their views, permission and community agreements must not be invented or replaced by simulated stakeholder responses.

The priority of these additional roles is not settled. The value of a role or use case does not by itself establish that it is the intended primary audience.

## Intended audience versus current access path

The product's broader audience need not be limited to developers. Its current manual route uses an existing AI assistant that can read the skill folder and relative references. Existing agent workflows inform the chosen initial audience, but tool familiarity alone does not establish the contribution/support need or measured adoption.

The host's privacy, cost and actual file/tool capabilities still apply. A portable package or a supported installer target does not establish accessibility, activation or usefulness for the intended audience. Distribution choices should serve the audience rather than define it by whichever tool is easiest to package.

## Positioning language to retain as an option

For the initial audience:

> ItF helps agent-native builders put their energy and experience towards real problems, substantiate their contribution, and connect with peers, communities and potential supporters.

For the broader audience:

> ItF helps people doing socially useful work clarify how they contribute, learn what is working, and make an honest case for support.

These are positioning candidates, not adopted taglines or claims that the current skill already delivers connections or financial support. They do not promise funding, independently verified impact or better outcomes merely because a document was produced.

## One important job: employment and income insecurity

One important situation is people losing jobs and struggling to get hired, with AI agents among the pressures on demand for their paid work. Someone may have strong skills and increased technical capacity while facing less employment security. Being proficient with the tools does not remove that threat. This is one entry point to ItF, not a universal description of agent-native builders or the sole reason to use it.

This recognises a material problem; it is not a measured prevalence or causal estimate of AI-related job losses. It does not attribute every hiring difficulty to agents or establish willingness to use ItF.

Do not euphemise employment and income insecurity as merely abundant building capacity, a lack of purpose or a weak funding narrative. Do not assume that a displaced worker wants to become an entrepreneur, launch a SaaS product or prove social impact. A person's entitlement to dignity and a livelihood does not depend on making an exceptional impact claim.

ItF's possible contribution is narrower: for someone who chooses this route, help identify useful work, distinguish actual contribution from output, organise existing evidence and make an honest case for support outside or alongside conventional employment. That may be relevant to open-source maintenance, shared infrastructure or community contributions whose value is not straightforward to sell. It is not an established replacement for employment, a job-placement service, income protection or a guaranteed route to customers, funders or funding. A better document alone does not solve the livelihood problem.

The needs to choose worthwhile work, connect with peers and communities, learn what helps and explain what support enables also matter independently of unemployment. Their importance follows from the person's situation; they are not substitutes for addressing income insecurity when it is present. Whether ItF can provide material help across these jobs remains an open usefulness question.

This positioning note is not based on audience interviews, recruitment or collected user feedback.
