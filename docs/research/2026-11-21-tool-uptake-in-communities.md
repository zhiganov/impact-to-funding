# What gets a tool taken up by communities, and how to tell it stuck

Research from 2026-11-21. It asks what has worked to get a practice or tool like ItF taken up and passed on by communities, and how to know whether it lasted. It's meant for ItF's own uptake, and for builders spreading their projects.

**Sources.** All findings come from [Hey Nabu](../hey-nabu-assessment.md)'s Reaching People expert, asked on 2026-11-21 (server version 0.17.0). Each entry is Hey Nabu's own summary of a source, read on the date it gives. None of the linked pages was opened for this note. Most evidence comes from training programmes, farming and campaigns, not software. Treat it as analogy for a tool like ItF.

## Short answer

- **People hear about a practice from peers.** Whether they take it up can depend on hearing it from more than one.
- **Trained people pass it on only if someone supports them afterwards.** Most people trained to teach a practice never do.
- **An open licence lets more people run a tool without its maker.**
- **The field rarely measures whether people keep going,** so "did it stick" needs a measure you set yourself.

## Peers carry it, sometimes more than once

- In Indian villages, a microfinance participant was seven times as likely as a non-participant to tell another household. Whether the teller had taken it up made no difference to whether the listener did. The trust that moved people was in hearing about it, not in a peer's example.
- Some practices need several contacts before people adopt them; some need one:
  - **Needed several:** in 200 Malawian villages, extension workers chose which farmers to train first in a planting method. After three years, 45% of those villages had no other adopter. Choosing first farmers so others would hear from two of them left another adopter in 85% of villages by the third season, against 54%.
  - **One was enough:** scholars moving from Twitter/X to Bluesky mostly followed a single contact.
- **The question to ask:** is your practice costly or uncertain enough that people want to hear from several others first? If so, start with a group who know each other, not people scattered widely. The field evidence for that choice is narrower than the claim: in Malawi, overall adoption rates across the seeding methods couldn't be told apart.

## Training people to pass it on

- **Most trainees never teach:**
  - Climate for Change trained over 280 facilitators. 133 ran at least one conversation, and 18 ran about half.
  - In Scotland's Choose Life programme, only a quarter of trained facilitators trained anyone. Those who did had supervision.
  - In one English health programme, 4.4% of trainers reported passing the training on.
- **Support after training is what changes this:**
  - Climate for Change's reach peaked while it ran a mentor programme for new facilitators.
  - Transition Network moved recognition of its trainers to a circle of trainers with a community of practice and a part-time coordinator, after training stalled. Its community lists 74 certified trainers from 22 countries.
  - La Fresque du Climat mentors new facilitators through their first workshops, and has more than 100,000 facilitators.
- **More trainers, more teachers:** The Carpentries' founder trained every instructor himself until 2016. After it trained more trainers, newly certified instructors rose from 464 to 625 a year (2017 to 2020). About half of trainees certify, and 28% never begin the last steps.
- **Count active people, not certificates:** En-ROADS counts an ambassador as active only after two events a year.
- **Volunteer leaders worked better than self-run groups:** Global Action Plan tested three ways to run its EcoTeams household groups. The self-run version didn't change behaviour. The version led by trained volunteers changed it most and cost less. It has reached over 4,000 UK households.

## Who may run it

A maker that licenses its tool only to people it has trained keeps every use behind its permission, so fewer people can run it alone. Hey Nabu marks this as disputed, and it rests on one maker's licence terms. The counter-examples are open:
- **Carbon Literacy:** anyone certified can teach, and the first level of trainer accreditation is free.
- **La Fresque du Climat:** anyone who has attended a workshop can take a short, pay-what-you-choose facilitator training. It funds itself with a 10% royalty on paid commercial use.

## How to tell it stuck

The field mostly doesn't measure this. Hey Nabu found nobody researching these, among others:
- years a trained teacher stays active;
- support given to a new teacher after training;
- share of adopters who would recommend a practice to people they know;
- share of people who stay with a practice once it's the default.

The nearest published measures come from giving:
- About half of one-time online donors to US nonprofits in 2024 gave again in 2025 (M+R Benchmarks). 71% of monthly donors were still giving a year later.
- Across 17,000 organisations, 90% of donors who gave seven or more times were kept, against 14.4% of one-time donors (Fundraising Effectiveness Project).

## What this suggests for ItF

These are suggestions drawn from the evidence above, not findings about ItF:

- **Spread through groups that already meet,** such as guilds and community dashboards (#102, my-community#140), so newcomers hear about ItF from several people at once.
- **Support the people who help others use it.** A small circle or community of practice for people who've installed ItF for a group may matter more than more docs.
- **Keep it open.** ItF's MIT and CC BY-SA licences already let anyone run and adapt it without asking.
- **Define "active" before counting:** for example, someone who finishes a second task, or helps one other person set it up. Count that, not installs.
- **Check back after three months,** using the [feedback guide](../user-feedback.md), and record who is still using it.

## Gaps

- The evidence is about teaching practices, farming and campaigns. Hey Nabu holds little on software tools spreading through communities.
- Whether a skill like ItF spreads on one contact or several is unknown.
- This note covers one question on one day.
