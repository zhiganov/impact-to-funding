# How 360Giving won funder adoption

Research from 2026-11-21. It asks how 360Giving, a UK open standard for grants data, got hundreds of funders to publish. It's meant as a precedent for [Funding to Impact (FtI)](https://itfti.org/fti), which asks funders to publish a file about their priorities and calls.

**Sources.** Most findings come from [Hey Nabu](../hey-nabu-assessment.md), asked on 2026-11-21 (server version 0.17.0), from its Systemic Philanthropy and System Change experts. Each entry is Hey Nabu's own summary of a source, read on the date it gives. The founding history comes from a web search on the same day. None of the linked pages was opened for this note.

## Short answer

360Giving grew from a few funders in 2015 to about 355 by October 2026. The pattern behind that growth:

- **Funders started it.** A funder convened other funders and technical experts. One of the largest UK funders published its entire back catalogue early.
- **Publishing is free and well supported.** There are free workshops, a helpdesk, one-to-one help and a checking tool.
- **Funders get something back.** Their grants appear in a shared search, and they can compare their giving with other funders'.
- **The UK government uses it.** It tells its own grant-makers to use the standard.

It took ten years, and keeping files current is still the weak point.

## How it started (web)

- **The founder was a funder.** [Fran Perrin](https://www.threesixtygiving.org/about/our-history) couldn't find the information she needed for funding decisions. In 2014 she gathered like-minded funders and technical experts to build a shared format. She later gave the UK state's thin publication of grants data as a reason. 360Giving was constituted in July 2015.
- **Early backers:** the steering group was set up by the Indigo Trust with the Big Lottery Fund, Nominet Trust and Nesta. Early support also came from Esmée Fairbairn Foundation.
- **First publishers, 2015 to 2016:** the Big Lottery Fund published all its grants since 2004. [BBC Children in Need and Comic Relief](https://fundraising.co.uk/2016/08/17/360giving-receives-lottery-funding-boost/) followed. The Big Lottery Fund also gave 360Giving a £50,000 development grant in November 2015.

| July 2025, ten years on | Over 320 grantmakers | Over 1 million grants |
| October 2026 | About 355 funders on the home page | 2 million grants, £338bn |

- **Government:** 17 to 18 UK government departments publish. Hey Nabu reports that the UK government tells its own grant-makers to use the standard. The UK government lists it as an [open standard for grant-making](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/open-standards-for-government/data-standard-for-grant-making).
- **Outside the UK:** the research found no open grants standard adopted in the USA or Canada. US funders report voluntarily to Candid instead. A 2012 US Reporting Commitment had 15 large foundations publishing quarterly.

## What makes it work

- **Free to publish.** The main cost is staff time. Publishing takes five stages: plan, prepare, publish, post-publish and re-publish. The guidance says it more often takes weeks or months than days.
- **Free support:** written guidance, a helpdesk, free workshops such as "Introduction to publishing", and one-to-one help. 360Giving charges only for consultancy that benefits a single organisation, such as benchmarking.
- **A checking tool and a dashboard.** The Data Quality Checker tests a file against the standard. The Data Quality Dashboard tracks publishers and data quality.
- **Something back for funders:**
  - **GrantNav and an API** make every grant searchable.
  - **The annual UKGrantmaking report** analyses the data alongside charity regulator figures.
  - **Recognition:** publishers say they value comparing their grants with other funders', and being seen as transparent.
- **Governance:** a voluntary Stewardship Committee oversees changes to the standard, with Open Data Services Co-operative as technical partner. Since 1 February 2026, 360Giving is part of Funders Together, a funders' infrastructure body. The committee continues.

## Where it struggles

- **Files go stale.** Of 297 publishers in the registry on 25 October 2026, 171 had updated a file in the previous twelve months, and 64 hadn't updated one in two years. The publisher count includes anyone who ever published. Another reading the next day counted 192 who had published in the last twelve months.
- **Optional fields stay sparse:**
  - 95% of grants carry a programme title;
  - 41% carry a duration;
  - 23% carry a classification;
  - 70% use an external organisation identifier, which is what lets one recipient's grants be matched across funders.
- **Nobody tracks how a grant was decided.** The standard has an optional field, "From An Open Call?". The dashboard doesn't track how often it's filled.
