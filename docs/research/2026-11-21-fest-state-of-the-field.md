# FEST State of the Field 2026

A reading note from 2026-11-21 on the [FEST State of the Field 2026](https://stateofthefield.festfield.finance/) report, and what it means for ItF and [Funding to Impact (FtI)](https://itfti.org/fti).

**Source.** The report's web page, read in full on 2026-11-21, apart from the individual reflections and the interactive maps. Quotes are the report's own words. Figures are the report's own, and claims are participants' views, not checked facts.

## What it is

FEST (Financial Ecosystems for Systemic Transformation) is a community working on "financing systemic transformation": changing how private, public and philanthropic money reaches social, economic and ecological change. This is its first annual report.

- **Who took part:** 123 people from 23 countries and territories, naming 188 organisations. They contributed through a registration form, small "weaving" calls in June 2026 and a World Café call on 18 June 2026.
- **What's in it:**
  - reflections from FEST's stewards, Steve Waddell and Jen van der Meer;
  - a "collective narrative" of 15 themes, clustered by hand from participants' report-outs;
  - a map of who is connected to whom: over 80% of contributors connect through shared affiliations;
  - eight roles drawn from how contributors describe their own work, including weaver, sensemaker, capacity cultivator, infrastructure steward and financial catalyst.
- **How to read it:** the report calls itself "a snapshot rather than a comprehensive or representative survey". The narrative is "a sensing tool", not an agreed position.
- **What's next:** calls twice a year, in June to gather input for the next report, and in November to discuss this one.

## Findings that matter here

**Who's in the room.**
- Jen van der Meer notes that "nobody described themselves as sitting on the assets". No pension fund, endowment, insurer or family principal appears among the affiliations. Most participants work "next to, underneath, or around a funding or finance decision rather than making one".
- Steve Waddell notes that government got little attention, and that foundations and other legacy institutions are underrepresented.

**Money is too small, and its rules are hard to see.** Participants were frustrated by "comically low numbers ($5K–$2.5 million, as named by the bioregional movements)" against the scale needed. They were also frustrated "with the misunderstanding of the constraints of those tasked with capital allocation". The report sees the frustration running both ways: little understanding of the rules allocators work under, and little patience from finance with newcomers' language.

**The field is hard to join.** Theme 13 names "a complexity problem that makes it hard to grasp the field or join it", and asks how to keep many views while building a shared story.

**Shared languages, not one language.** Theme 4 calls for "building interoperable languages, not just expecting consensus in one unified language".

**Trust is built in person, over time.** Theme 14 says trust grows through in-person events, and through relationships across many kinds of actors.

**Other recurring themes:**
- **Place-based work leads:** bioregional, community-led and Indigenous-led ways of moving money, cooperatives, and "living labs".
- **Risk is mispriced:** participants want new mandates to "rightsize the risk".
- **Distribution is unequal:** one example was a system in Rwanda built for the most vulnerable that came to work for almost everyone.
- **Payout is low:** one participant said "only 5% of foundation money gets distributed". That reflects the US minimum payout rule; the report doesn't check it.

## What this suggests for ItF and FtI

These are suggestions drawn from the report, not findings about ItF or FtI:

- **FtI explains allocators' rules to the people asking.** A funder file that says what the money may pay for, who decides and how people are found is one answer to the "misunderstanding of the constraints" this report names. It also suits the call for "interoperable languages": FtI reuses CommonGrants rather than inventing another schema.
- **FEST is a convenor route, not a funder cluster.** Its members are mostly intermediaries, advisers and builders, not people who control funds. For FtI's [adoption plan](2026-11-21-what-this-means-for-fti.md), FEST is a place to learn what intermediaries need from funder information, and to reach funders through them.
- **ItF fits the "infrastructure steward" role.** The report's complexity problem is the one ItF works on for builders: plain language about impact and funding.
- **The next chance to take part is January 2027.** The report was presented at FEST's November call, around 2026-11-19. The next gathering is expected in January 2027, earlier than the June and November schedule the report describes.

## What Hey Nabu holds on FEST and TransCap

Asked on 2026-11-21 (server version 0.17.0). Each entry is Hey Nabu's own summary of a source, read on the date it gives.

- **FEST** grew out of Catalyst 2030's Working Group 11. In 2023 the community agreed four qualities that define the field, including organising whole financing systems rather than single funds. It says it works on redesign: linking maturing prototypes. It's one of four anchor organisations of the Systemic Investing Field Mapping initiative, launched in November 2025 with TransCap, TWIST and Deep Transitions Lab. The consultancy Omplexity supports that mapping. ([FEST home page](https://www.festfield.finance/), read in part on 2026-09-14)
- **[TransCap Initiative](https://transformation.capital/)** is a Swiss non-profit that builds the field of "systemic investing": funding one system's change with several kinds of money under one strategy. It calls the coordinating body a "financial backbone".
  - **Projects:** it's designing a capital orchestrator for regenerative agriculture in the US Midwest. A podcast summary describes $1.4 billion in catalytic capital for it.
  - **Publications:** a [practice guide](https://transcap-initiative.gitbook.io/systemic-investing-practice-guide/introduction/about-systemic-investing), case studies, and a November 2026 [playbook](https://medium.com/transformation-capital/thinking-about-bringing-systemic-investing-to-your-country-d4e38ddd7a6c) for starting in a new country: phased 12-to-18-month experiments led by an orchestrator and four or five core partners.
  - **Events:** it runs the annual [Systemic Investing Summit](https://systemicinvestingsummit.org/about/). The 2026 summit was in Rio de Janeiro on 14 to 16 April, with about 250 people, at $700 a ticket.
  - **Evidence:** Hey Nabu notes that most evidence on financial backbones is TransCap's own account of work it designed or admires.
- **One of Hey Nabu's tracked measures** fits FtI closely: "understanding among holders of different kinds of capital of each other's constraints and language". It's the same gap the FEST report names.
- **No critique found.** Hey Nabu found no direct critique of systemic investing or capital orchestration. The nearest are general critiques of blended finance and de-risking, such as Mariana Mazzucato's.
