# Optional framework lenses for a theory of change

Use this reference only when a framework could help the current decision or the
person asks for one. Ordinary ItF use needs no named framework. These are original
ItF selection notes and prompts, not official worksheets, complete assessments,
certification or endorsement by the framework authors. No diagrams or datasets
are bundled. See [sources and rights](../SOURCES.md).

## Choose a lens, not a compulsory worldview

- Start with the actual decision, subject, stage and scale. Reuse a useful existing
  local/community model within its actual access and reuse basis. Do not extract
  restricted knowledge into a public reference library.
- If no framework would change the decision, continue the ordinary skill workflow.
  Do not interrupt an assessment with a mandatory framework menu or long catalogue.
  If suggesting a lens, explain its specific use and limits; the person may decline.
- The two candidates below are a small starter, not an exhaustive or ranked library.
  They may expose omissions; neither supplies local need, consent or causal evidence.
- Keep personal purpose, shared project theory and bounded contribution separate.
  A personal theory or its disclosure is never required to use a project lens.
  A framework-informed project revision is a proposal until actually agreed.

## Translate only what helps the theory

1. Identify the lens/source edition actually used and why it is useful here.
2. Ask a few relevant questions about the causal pathway, missing perspectives,
   trade-offs or dependencies. Do not fill every dimension or invent indicators.
3. Keep activities, outputs, outcomes and intended impact distinct. Explain the
   mechanism, assumptions, alternatives, evidence and what could change the theory.
4. State any global-to-local translation, data and value/allocation assumptions.
   Unsupported numerical translation stays unestablished; qualitative inquiry is
   valid. A global framework fact is not a project observation.
5. Say what changed, what remains unknown and the next useful decision. Supporting
   existing work, declining the lens, revising or stopping may be appropriate.

Three short lines may suffice: **lens/source and purpose; relevant question and
scale limit; resulting ToC change or unresolved decision**. No extra form, public
profile, life mission or new evidence collection is required. AI can assist inquiry
and drafting, not invent affected people's views or decide on their behalf.

## Candidate: Planetary Boundaries

**Reference:** [Stockholm Resilience Centre overview](https://www.stockholmresilience.org/research/planetary-boundaries.html),
checked 2026-11-05. It describes nine interdependent Earth-system processes and
global risk boundaries. Its displayed current graphic is labelled a 2026 update;
that is not a complete version identifier for every underlying study or dataset.

**Useful for:** environmental context, dependencies and possible pressure shifting
in a proposed pathway. Original ItF questions include:

- Which relevant environmental pressure might this activity change, through what
  mechanism and compared with what alternative?
- Could a reduction in one pressure shift burdens elsewhere or worsen another?
- What local conditions, system boundary and evidence would make this claim useful?

**Limits:** global control variables do not automatically become personal/project
quotas or local safe thresholds. Numerical downscaling needs an explicit method,
units, relevant place/time, system boundary, data, uncertainty and ethical allocation
assumptions. Equal population or spending shares are not neutral defaults. Legitimate
downscaling is possible, but this starter supplies no such method or calculation.
A repair count or emissions estimate does not establish compliance with all nine
boundaries, exclusive contribution, or a funding entitlement.

**Rights:** the inspected overview's displayed 2026 Planetary Boundaries graphics
are CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 with their stated credits, not MIT. This is not a licence for
every linked study or dataset. Link to the source; do not import, modify, translate or redraw those
graphics as part of this starter. Any later asset use needs its own rights check.

## Candidate: Doughnut Economics

**References:** DEAL's [Principles & Guidelines](https://doughnuteconomics.org/principles-and-guidelines)
and [The Evolving Doughnut](https://doughnuteconomics.org/tools/the-evolving-doughnut),
checked 2026-11-05. The latter describes the 2025 iteration/report; its page-update
date, 2026-10-20, is different from the source edition and underlying observation
periods. The existing [four-lenses guidance](https://doughnuteconomics.org/tools/doughnut-unrolled-dimensions-of-the-four-lenses)
relates local/global and social/ecological perspectives for places. Its page
description was inspected through search results; linked slide decks were not read.

**Useful for:** keeping social needs and ecological limits together, and noticing
distribution and wider consequences instead of only counting local outputs.
Original ItF questions include:

- Whose needs might this specific contribution help, and who may be excluded or
  bear costs? Whose actual perspective is missing?
- What ecological dependencies or effects beyond the immediate project could
  change the proposed mechanism or choice of activity?
- What would support or challenge this pathway, rather than merely label it aligned?

**Limits:** retain both social foundation and ecological ceiling when describing
the framework. A partial inquiry is not a complete Doughnut assessment, a personal
score or evidence that a project/place meets all conditions. Simulated stakeholder
views cannot supply local judgement or consent. Consult relevant context-specific
DEAL guidance before claiming to implement its approach; no conformance is claimed
by using these questions. These prompts are not a copied/adapted DEAL worksheet.

**Rights:** the inspected guidelines license platform tools under CC BY-SA 4.0,
with asset-specific scope and exceptions; external content and personal stories
are not automatically covered. The Evolving Doughnut diagrams have specified
CC BY-SA 4.0 credits, not MIT terms. Trademarks, logos and branding remain separate;
DEAL also asks users not to generate Doughnut diagrams with AI. This starter links
sources without importing tools, diagrams or stories. A later actual adaptation
must keep the applicable attribution/share-alike terms, not be relabelled MIT.

## Evidence, versions and updates

Use [claim review](claims.md), [contribution relationships](contributions.md) and
the usual reporting/disclosure rules. Framework alignment is not an observed
outcome, independent review, source-author endorsement or grant eligibility.
Never add shared results or costs again because two lenses/projects mention them.

Keep the source edition/date where known separate from the retrieval date, this
reference's revision and an assessment's version. A checked link is not immutable
or proof of current data. If the source is inaccessible or its edition/rights are
unclear, state the limit; do not invent provenance or fetch restricted material.
Use only relevant supplied/authorised material, not a compulsory live data feed.

For source changes, review affected notes/prompts/rights explicitly. Do not rewrite
past intentions, silently migrate shared theories or transfer earlier scoped reviews
to new claims. Keep only permitted correction/history. These are dated starter
references maintained through ordinary repository contributions, not an exhaustive
catalogue, automatic monitor, staffed commons or promised update service. No MCP
server or native skill discovery is needed for manual file-reading use.

The [fictional examples](../examples/framework-lenses.md) illustrate intended use,
not observed model behaviour or evidence that these prompts are reliable.
