# Personal direction, shared projects and bounded contributions

Use this reference when exploring a person's direction, developing a contribution with
others, connecting an individual to shared project work or reviewing a portfolio. It is
original ItF guidance, not an official Moral Ambition method.

## Explore worthwhile directions and real needs

When direction is open, help connect what the person can offer and cares about with
needs defined by the people affected. Allow several possibilities and a purpose that
changes over time; don't assign a life mission. Keep personal direction separate from
any shared project's purpose. A plausible fit with an existing effort isn't an
invitation or an agreed role.

Identify affected people, who defines success, benefits, costs, exclusions and missing
perspectives. Apply the Moral Ambition framing to a **specific gap**:

- Importance: how much does this problem matter to those affected?
- Neglectedness: what remains unmet after existing responses and access barriers?
- Fixability: what could this person or team realistically change with their capacity?

A large global problem doesn't establish a local need or an effective intervention.
Compare supporting existing work, a simpler approach and doing nothing new. Keep
hard-to-measure benefits, relationship quality, agency, systemic change and future
effects in view. Don't invent a numerical comparison without a defensible common basis.

## Develop a contribution with others

Help describe a bounded contribution: what the person does or could offer, how it
serves a real need, who else contributes, and what capacity or support it takes.
Maintenance, care, coordination and enabling others can be meaningful work. Don't
favour new projects, visible output or easily counted benefits.

Where collaboration matters, explore known peers, communities, existing efforts, shared
interests, missing perspectives and ways to take part. Keep an existing relationship,
an unverified possibility and an agreed commitment distinct. You can help prepare a
private conversation or collaboration outline. Don't promise matches, invent
availability or make networking a condition of contributing.

If the person wants a mentor, help them think of people and communities they already
know who could help, and prepare a short request in their own words: the question,
the kind of help (a one-off question, an office hour or a short relationship) and only
the context they choose to share. ItF keeps no mentor pool, so don't suggest one exists
or offer introductions. Mentoring advice isn't claim review or endorsement. See
[mentor pool scoping](../docs/mentor-pool-scoping.md).

Keep personal direction, shared project theories and bounded contributions distinct. A
portfolio connects selected contributions, relationships and learning. It doesn't need
one unified mission, duplicate project theories or private personal purposes. Keep known
capacity conflicts, unavailable theories and version-specific evidence without inventing
alignment or credit. The sections below give the detail.

## Select the useful view, not a mandatory hierarchy

| View | What to explain | What not to require or infer |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | The changes the person wants to contribute to and why their chosen work might help. | No compulsory unified life mission, project affiliation or disclosure of private motivations. Several pathways or an uncertain direction are legitimate. |
| Project | The shared change pathway, assumptions, responsibilities and whose agreement is actually recorded. | No personal theories from contributors. Authorship, membership or a supplied draft does not establish collective approval. |
| Contribution | A specific actual or proposed role connecting a person to a project pathway. | Not ownership of the project, all its outcomes, its reviews or its funding authority. |
| Portfolio | A decision-focused view across the person's selected contributions and personal pathways. | Not five personal theories for five projects, an impact total, a public roster or a required database. |

People and projects have many-to-many relationships. A project can appear within
a personal portfolio without becoming subordinate to that person's purpose.
Contributors can have different motivations while agreeing to a bounded project
role. Project experience may change personal direction; proposed project changes
do not become collective decisions without actual agreement. Do not demand a
complete portfolio or personal theory to assess one contribution.

## Connect only what is known and useful

Reuse an existing shared project theory at its source. Refer to its relevant
version or dated supplied excerpt rather than copying it into each assessment.
If the source has no stable version, record the dated excerpt actually relied
on within permitted retention, or state that the reference may have changed; a
live link alone is not a version.
Identify supplied versus inspected material and provisional versus approved text.
If no project theory is available, describe the known role and mark the pathway
unknown; a clearly labelled provisional hypothesis can help if requested, but
must not impersonate an approved shared theory or invent stakeholder consent.

For each relevant contribution, use concise prose or the optional assessment
section to explain:

- Person/chosen identity, project and available theory/pathway reference/version.
- Actual or proposed role, period and limited responsibility; separate work done
  from intentions and observed results from expected benefits.
- Why the contribution fits, explores or challenges a personal pathway, only if
  useful and voluntarily supplied. Withheld motivations are not missing evidence
  that must be collected before a project assessment can proceed.
- Mechanism, dependencies, actual capacity/effort where known and what could fail.
- Claim/evidence references, their access limits, other contributors and uncertainty.
- The next decision, changed belief or useful review condition, if warranted.

Unknown roles, dates, capacity, theory versions, approvals and evidence stay
unknown. Do not create accounts, records or sensitive forms merely to complete
these prompts. One short section can express the relationship.

## Compare contributions without manufacturing coherence or credit

Show reinforcing work, duplicate activity, competing commitments and uncertain
fit. Compare known commitments with supplied capacity; do not allocate unknown
time, assume unreported projects do not exist, prescribe a moral ranking or
force every project into one mission. A prospective contribution can be revised,
paused or declined when commitments conflict.

A shared output can appear by reference in both personal and project reports,
but it remains the same shared work. Reusing one resource across projects is not
independent creation in each project. Keep joint outcomes attributed rather than
adding them repeatedly. Do not invent causal-credit percentages or turn effort
shares into impact shares. A project's success does not prove every contributor's
causal effect; a person's completed task does not prove the project worked.

For funding, distinguish each ask's actual marginal work and costs, existing
support and overlap across personal and project asks. The same drafting cost
cannot be requested twice because two projects use the draft. Different genuine
adaptation costs may be separate, if supported. Unknown support is not zero;
neither a portfolio link nor permission to publish establishes financial authority.

## Preserve versions, dissent and disclosure

Personal and project reports can reference the same evidence, within the uses
its custodian has permitted for each report, while making different, bounded
claims. Apply the [claim rules](claims.md): an actual review
remains bound to the exact claim and evidence version examined. A revised theory,
reused output or new portfolio summary does not inherit that review. Preserve
earlier intentions and explain what changed rather than rewriting past alignment.
Historical contributions do not imply current membership or continuing endorsement.

Keep private motivations, identities and project associations private. Even a
project name or reference may reveal a protected relationship. Do not fetch
restricted material without an access basis, expose private locators, merge
identities or publish a complete portfolio by default. If relevant evidence cannot
be disclosed, explain its limits without revealing the protected association.

A public candidate needs its own exact content/audience approval under the
[funding and disclosure guidance](funding-and-review.md). Project-only reports
need not include personal theories, and a curated personal report may omit
restricted projects without misleadingly implying the remainder is exhaustive.
No account, group, database, hosted service or protocol integration is required.
