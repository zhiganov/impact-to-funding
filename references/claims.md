# Reviewing claims without manufacturing certainty

This is an original proposed review convention, not an accredited standard or an official Moral Ambition methodology. Use it for decision support, not as a score or certification ladder.

## Separate claim type, support and review provenance

| Dimension | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Claim type | Activity/output; observed outcome; causal/contribution claim; forecast/proposed outcome; assumption; or financial statement. Split mixed claims. |
| Support | Supported within stated scope; partly supported; insufficient; conflicting; or not assessed. Explain the evidence and limitation; these labels are not measured probabilities. |
| Review provenance | Self-report; AI-assisted examination; documented peer/partner review; or documented independent evaluation. Record what actually happened, not what is requested or intended. |
| Scope and currency | Subject, role, affected group, place if necessary/safe, observation/reporting period, evidence date, review date, source access and appropriate recheck conditions. |

An independent reviewer can make a weak assessment. A detailed self-report can support a narrow observation. Neither reviewer identity nor label automatically establishes causal validity. Where independence, authenticity or identity is unknown, say so.

Call a review independent only with a recorded basis that the reviewer is separate from the reviewed work and its material interests: authorship or coauthorship (including on another project sharing the work), team or close ties, who funds or selects the reviewer, and outcome-contingent payment. Being outside the project under review is not enough. Otherwise record partner review or independence unknown.

## Minimum useful claim record

Use stable local identifiers when several claims/sources must be cross-referenced. Do not require a database or published schema.

- Claim statement and type; subject/person/project and the individual's actual role.
- Relevant group, scope and time period; the measured quantity's definition, numerator, denominator and missingness for counts/rates where relevant.
- Evidence identifiers, descriptions, source/observation dates and what was actually inspected.
- Provenance and access limits: supplied account, observed source content, corroboration or unavailable material.
- Support assessment, reasoning, contradictory evidence and uncertainty.
- Other contributors, pre-existing work, plausible alternatives and what likely happens without this contribution.
- Actual reviewer, role, review date, method, interests, independence limits and reference/version, if an actual review exists.
- Disclosure basis and any material correction, dispute or supersession.

Do not expose private source identifiers or sensitive evidence links in a public version. A public statement can explain that supporting material is restricted without pretending it is publicly auditable. Never tell the user private evidence must become public to be meaningful.

## Reasoning checks

1. Does the source support the exact wording, population, period and magnitude? A citation beside a stronger claim is not support.
2. Was the source inspected, or merely linked, quoted by someone else or unavailable? Search snippets and incomplete extracts have limited coverage.
3. Is the outcome defined independently of the activity? Attendance, code commits and introductions do not prove improved wellbeing, decisions or resilience.
4. Was change observed against a relevant baseline? If the groups/periods differ, preserve that limitation.
5. What else could explain the result? Consider prior plans, external trends, selection, other actors and displaced activity. Do not invent a counterfactual estimate.
6. Who disagrees or is missing? Affected people supply judgement and knowledge, not just testimonials for a predetermined story.
7. Are benefits shared and costs/harms visible? Do not count the whole project's result as the person's exclusive impact.
8. What evidence would make the claim weaker or change the plan? Do not discard it to improve fundraising copy.

If the decision does not justify more evidence, preserve uncertainty rather than expanding collection. Strong causal/financial/legal claims may require qualified review outside this skill's capacity.

## Derived measures must retain their definitions

Before calculating a rate, complement or change, establish what the source actually counts, the numerator/denominator, the periods compared, and how missing, cancelled, duplicate or unclassified records are handled. Keep that definition in the conclusion. Correct arithmetic does not establish a different outcome category or causal meaning.

For example, scheduled appointments minus recorded completions yields appointments **not recorded as completed**, not necessarily missed appointments or no-shows. Cancellations, rescheduling and recording gaps may be included unless the source defines otherwise. Report the recorded completion measure, or its explicitly labelled complement, and leave the unmeasured classification unknown. Likewise, nonresponse is not proof of an unmet need or a negative outcome.

Distinguish a percentage-point difference from a relative percentage change, retain changed denominators and comparison limits, and do not turn either into an attributable effect. Avoid unnecessary derived figures when the source counts communicate the useful finding more clearly.

## What verification can and cannot establish

- A receipt can support expenditure, not the benefit of that expenditure.
- A signed account record can support account-origin/integrity, not truth, human uniqueness or reviewer independence.
- A partner account can corroborate an observed arrangement without proving causality or serving as an independent evaluator.
- An AI consistency check is not an independent audit, authenticity check or accreditation.
- Self-reported readiness, satisfaction or increased confidence are perspectives, not proof of preparedness or better decisions.
- A forecast or target is not an observation. A realised donation is not a social outcome.
- No adverse event occurring is not proof that the intervention prevented it.

## Updates, reviews and disputes

Tie each actual review to the precise claim and evidence version it examined. A changed claim does not inherit a prior attestation. Retain the previous claim and explain a correction/supersession when relevant; do not rewrite history to hide failure.

Distinguish a withdrawn review from a change in permission to use evidence. Mark a withdrawn review as such and do not present it as a current endorsement. Withdrawal alone does not make the underlying claim false or remove support from other permitted evidence; reassess support if new contrary evidence is supplied.

For changed evidence permissions, establish which material, audience and use are affected. Stop affected uses while the actual permission/retention basis is checked; do not assume all uses or historical records are revoked. Narrow or omit a dependent claim where the remaining permitted evidence cannot support its wording for that audience. Permission for private assessment is not permission for public quotation, export or fundraising.

Retain only authorised historical review/correction information, with withdrawal and version limits explicit. A permitted historical reference is not a current endorsement or permission to reproduce restricted material. Do not erase legitimate history by default, retain prohibited content for an audit trail, or imply that local correction removes others' copies.

No response to a review request means no attestation, not implicit approval. Do not contact a reviewer without authorisation. Distinguish a public source from permission to represent someone as endorsing a project.

Keep material conflicts and disputes visible in the private assessment. A private allegation is not automatically publishable fact. Resolve public wording fairly, with consent/privacy and the risk of harm considered; do not conceal a material limitation while claiming unqualified support.
