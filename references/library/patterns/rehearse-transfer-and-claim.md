# P003 — Rehearse the transfer and the claim

**Candidate pattern, record revision 1, prepared 2026-11-25.** An ItF interpretation
of three public teaching exercises. It hasn't been tested with people, it isn't a
required step in the skill, and the exercises' authors haven't endorsed ItF.

## Context and recurring problem

Someone has an analogy that seems to fit, or a result that looks good, and wants
to act on it. Reading more principles may not show them what they missed. A short
rehearsal on made-up material can catch a false match, a skewed sample or an
overstated cause before anyone's time, safety or money is at stake.

## Forces

- The same words can hide different relationships, purposes, incentives and people.
- A clear number makes it easy to miss who was left out, who disagreed, and how the
  person measuring shaped the result.
- A worked comparison helps someone revise, but it can look like the only right
  answer, or like proof of skill.
- Real situations involve consent, relationships and consequences that a solo
  exercise can't reproduce.

## Adaptable response

When it helps with a specific reasoning problem, offer a short dry run. The person
can skip it.

1. Use made-up material only.
2. Let the person try first, then compare their attempt with a worked alternative.
3. Include a flawed model or claim to fix, and a changed case that makes the first
   answer less comfortable.
4. Keep both the first attempt and the revision, and why it changed.

Pick only the rehearsal that fits:

- **Transferring an idea.** Compare relationships, not shared words. Write down one
  way the two situations match and one way they don't. Then propose a small
  intervention, and something you could observe that would show it's wrong.
- **Whose experience is missing.** Tell apart who's interested, who has influence,
  who's in control and who's affected. Check who was counted and how people were
  invited. Include accounts people can give easily, in proportion. Don't invent
  agreement, and don't read a declined invitation as having nothing to say.
- **Claiming a result.** Work out the change that was reported. Then say who it
  applies to, and whether it shows a cause. Keep the cases left out, the accounts
  that disagree, and the choices the evaluator made. Finish with what can fairly be
  said now, and one useful thing to learn next.

Where it fits, end with a question to test with real people. Don't turn the exercise
into a required assessment, and don't ask for real people's records.

## Source basis and relationship to the collection

Inspired by three exercises in The Necessary Tangle's practice pack, published
7 September 2026 and read on 2026-11-25:

- [Transposing theory](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/transposing-theory/):
  abstract a structure, record where it doesn't match, and return to the situation.
- [Participative inquiry](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/participative-inquiry/):
  check who a satisfaction claim sampled, and tell influence apart from being affected.
- [Evaluation and reflexivity](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/evaluation-and-reflexivity/):
  separate measured change from cause, keep negative accounts, and look at the
  evaluator's own influence.

The exercises have their own scenarios and answers. This note describes the teaching
arrangement they share. It doesn't reproduce their cases, and it doesn't claim their
authors support this pattern. The pack calls itself alpha material, not yet reviewed
by independent teaching specialists.

**Fits with [P001 revision 1](response-behind-the-channel.md):** a rehearsal can show
who could respond, and whose view is missing, before an arrangement is proposed. The
real arrangement still needs local authority and capacity.

**Fits with [P002 revision 1](keep-outcomes-distinct.md):** the claim rehearsal keeps
process, outcome, who benefits and side effects apart. It doesn't make the exercise
itself another outcome to measure.

**A different kind of record:** P001 and P002 are hypotheses drawn from historical
cases. P003 is a method, drawn from teaching exercises.

The optional [L001 revision 1](../lenses.md#l001--doughnut-economics) lens can help

## Consequences, failure conditions and alternatives

A dry run may show the analogy should be dropped or the claim narrowed. That's a good
outcome. A right answer shows only how someone handled the made-up material. It
doesn't show skill with real people, agreement, or impact. A real conversation,
existing records or a local method may serve better. Don't use the pattern to make
people complete a framework, hand in their answers, or gather more evidence than the
question needs.
