# P003 — Rehearse the transfer and the claim

**Candidate pattern, record revision 1, prepared 2026-11-25.** Method-inspired ItF
interpretation of three public exercises, not an empirically validated pattern,
a required skill workflow or a claim that the source authors endorse ItF.

## Context and recurring problem

A person has a plausible analogy or an appealing result and is tempted to act on
it too quickly. Reading more principles may not reveal what they overlooked. A
small fictional rehearsal can expose a mistaken correspondence, sampling boundary
or causal claim before real people's time, safety or resources are involved.

## Forces

- Similar words can hide different relationships, purposes, incentives and people.
- A clear metric can make excluded cases, contrary accounts and the evaluator's
  own influence easier to miss.
- A worked comparison can help someone revise, but can also look like the only
  permitted answer or a certificate of competence.
- Real situations involve consent, relationships and consequences that a solo
  exercise cannot reproduce.

## Adaptable response

Offer a short optional dry run when it serves a concrete reasoning problem.
Use wholly fictional material. Start with an attempt, then compare it with a
worked alternative. Include a defective model or claim to repair and a changed
case that makes the first answer less comfortable. Retain both the initial
attempt and the revision, with the reason for changing it.

Choose only the relevant rehearsal:

- **Transferring an idea:** abstract the relationships rather than the shared
  nouns. Record a correspondence and a non-correspondence. Derive a modest
  intervention and an observation that would challenge it.
- **Whose experience is missing:** distinguish interest, influence, control and
  being affected. Audit the denominator and invitation route. Proportionately
  include accessible accounts without manufacturing agreement or assuming that
  a declined invitation means a group has nothing to say.
- **Claiming a result:** calculate the reported change, then bound its population
  and causal meaning. Retain excluded cases, contrary accounts and the evaluator's
  choices. End with what can responsibly be said now and a useful learning action.

Stop with a question to test with people where appropriate. Do not turn the
exercise into a mandatory assessment or ask for identifiable real-world records.

## Source basis and relationship to the collection

This pattern is inspired by three exercises in The Necessary Tangle's practice
pack, published 7 September 2026 and inspected 2026-11-25:

- [Transposing theory](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/transposing-theory/):
  abstract a structure, record non-correspondence and return to the situation.
- [Participative inquiry](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/participative-inquiry/):
  audit a satisfaction claim's sampling boundary and distinguish influence from
  being affected.
- [Evaluation and reflexivity](https://transduction.systems/systems-thinking/practice/evaluation-and-reflexivity/):
  separate measured change from causation, retain negative accounts and examine
  the evaluator's influence.

The source exercises provide their own scenarios and comparisons. This note
interprets their shared teaching arrangement; it does not reproduce their cases,
claim their authors support this pattern or establish independent pedagogical
validation. The pack describes itself as alpha material with no independent
specialist pedagogical review.

**Complements [P001 revision 1](response-behind-the-channel.md):** rehearsal can
expose who can respond and whose perspective is missing before an arrangement
is proposed. The actual arrangement still needs local authority and capacity.
**Complements [P002 revision 1](keep-outcomes-distinct.md):** the claim rehearsal
keeps process, outcome, distribution and unintended effects separate. It does

## Consequences, failure conditions and alternatives

A dry run may reveal that the analogy should be abandoned or the claim narrowed.
That is useful. A correct answer establishes only how someone handled the
supplied material, not competence with real people, general agreement or impact.
A real conversation, existing records or a different local method may be more
useful. Do not use the pattern to make people complete a framework, submit their
answers or produce more evidence than the question needs.
