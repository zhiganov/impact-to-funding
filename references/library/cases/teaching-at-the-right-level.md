# C003 — Teaching at the Right Level in India

**Record revision 1; sources inspected 2026-11-08.** Domains: education,
foundational learning, public-service implementation. Historical tested delivery
packages, not a universal effect size or verification of all later scale-up.

## Context and theory provenance

Pratham's Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) addresses foundational learning gaps
among children who may be in higher grades without corresponding reading/arithmetic
skills. The inspected work concerns Indian implementations, principally children
in grades 3–5, as a complement to normal schooling rather than permanent tracking.

**Published programme mechanism; reconstructed short ToC:** assess foundational
learning → temporarily regroup by learning level → provide appropriate activities
and repeated assessment → improve basic skills. Protected instructional time,
practical support and delivery arrangements help make the approach an actual
practice. This chain is this note author's reconstruction from S1/S2, not a
separately inspected original pre-intervention ToC document.

## Findings and limits

- **S2 randomised evaluations:** delivery variants had materially different results.
  Haryana's supported government model improved reading; it targeted reading, so
  its null maths result is not a failed maths-treatment claim. Uttar Pradesh's
  intensive camps improved reading and maths in the tested settings.
- Some teacher-training/materials variants in Bihar and Uttarakhand did not show
  statistically significant learning gains. A Bihar volunteer-delivery variant
  did improve learning. These are not a single success/failure result for a state.
- The Uttar Pradesh materials-only arm had a small statistically significant maths
  effect but no significant language effect (S2 Table 3). Preserve this exception
  to the broad claim that materials alone never improve learning.
- Programme/process evidence suggests protected time and practical support helped
  sustain implementation. Trials tested bundles, not separate randomised effects
  of mentoring, regrouping, materials and time individually. The support explanation
  is a process interpretation, not proof of each component's universal necessity.

Studies differ in setting, baseline learning, attendance, delivery and data quality.
S2 reports baseline-data problems and differing attrition; these were not independently
reanalysed here. Findings concern basic learning during study follow-up, not every
educational goal, long-term earnings, permanent retention or all later beneficiaries.
Study-unit effect sizes should not be used as a cross-state or cross-domain ranking.

S1 is a synthesis by a long-term research/design partner. S2 explicitly discloses
that coauthor Rukmini Banerji, Pratham CEO, participated in design, implementation
and final review, but not collection/analysis. Funding included Hewlett Foundation,
3ie, Government of Haryana and CLEAR. This is not a wholly implementer-independent
author team. The inspected NBER working paper explicitly says it was not peer
reviewed as an official NBER publication; later journal publication does not
retroactively change the status of the inspected edition.

## Pattern connections and local questions

**ItF interpretation:** C003 revision 1 informs [P001 revision 1](../patterns/response-behind-the-channel.md)
through the need to make assessment information usable in actual teaching routines.
It informs [P002 revision 1](../patterns/keep-outcomes-distinct.md) through differences
between distributing materials, changing classroom practice and measured learning.
Neither link isolates a universal teaching or organisational mechanism.

Ask what instructors can actually do, what routines and authority support it,
whose needs the assessment recognises, and how adaptation affects inclusion.
Do not transplant particular camp schedules, assume unpaid volunteers are available,
or substitute test performance for all educational purposes.

Optional [L001/L002 questions, revision 1](../lenses.md): who receives useful teaching
and influence over it; who bears teaching/care costs; and which material/ecological
effects remain unassessed? No full framework implementation is inferred.
