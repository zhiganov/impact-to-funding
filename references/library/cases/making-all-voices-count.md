# C002 — Making All Voices Count

**Record revision 1; sources inspected 2026-11-08.** Domains: civic technology,
accountability, public-service relationships. **Theory and reported-learning case:**
the published theory and publisher summaries were inspected, not full evaluation
reports. No programme-wide effectiveness conclusion is supported here.

## Context and theory provenance

Making All Voices Count was a multi-country programme operating in 2013–2017,
implemented by Hivos, Ushahidi and the Institute of Development Studies (IDS).
Technology was intended to add value to wider citizen-voice and accountability
approaches, not replace those relationships.

**Published programme theory:** S1's June 2015 account describes grants/support →
innovation and research/learning → uptake by decision-makers, citizen/government
engagement and further innovation → improved relationships and dialogue/co-governance
→ contribution to people's ability to hold institutions to account. This is an
original paraphrase of contemporaneous published prose, not a reconstruction
presented as the original 2013 theory or a reproduced ToC diagram.

The theory itself asks about government responsiveness and exclusion. Those
questions and expected changes are not evidence that they occurred.

## Reported learning and limits

- **S2 publisher summary:** the final synthesis reappraises earlier technology
  optimism using programme research and experience. Its stated source volume is
  not a count of independent experiments or an impact estimate.
- **S3 publisher summary of a seven-project mHealth review:** reported findings
  emphasise existing community groups, health-worker involvement, allies able to
  change services, and offline spaces/relationships alongside digital feedback.
  The summary explicitly describes limits to technology without meaningful citizen
  engagement and relationships with government actors.
- These are the publisher's accounts of reported findings, not this note author's
  verification of the full reports' methods or underlying cases. No causal success
  rate, distributional effect, durable accountability gain or quantified harms
  estimate was verified. Responsiveness to a service issue is not automatically
  enforceable or sustained accountability.

IDS was a programme implementing/research partner. Programme funders included
Omidyar Network, Sida, UK aid/DFID and USAID. Full report-specific author interests,
reviewer relationships and detailed methods were not established. Do not label
the summaries independent evaluations or seven independent replications.

## Pattern connections and local questions

**ItF interpretation:** C002 revision 1 informs [P001 revision 1](../patterns/response-behind-the-channel.md)
through the reported need for relationships with actors able to respond, not
through proof of a universally sufficient response model. It informs
[P002 revision 1](../patterns/keep-outcomes-distinct.md) because a working feedback
channel or increased participation does not establish accountability.

For local adaptation, examine who is safe to speak, whose voice is filtered,
who has authority and incentives to act, and whether offline channels already
work better. Digital inclusion does not settle power, reprisal or institutional
capture. Do not assume community consensus or an available government ally.

Optional [L001/L002 questions, revision 1](../lenses.md): whose needs and influence
shape the channel; who bears participation/response costs; and what environmental
effects of the technical infrastructure remain unassessed? These are questions,
not claims that the programme implemented either framework.
