# C005 — Community-led total sanitation in rural Mali

**Record revision 1; sources inspected 2026-11-08.** Domains: public health,
sanitation, collective action. Mixed-results trial, not a general endorsement of
shame-based mobilisation, subsidy-free provision or health effects everywhere.

## Context and theory provenance

The Koulikoro sanitation directorate and UNICEF implemented community-led total
sanitation (CLTS) in small rural villages with relatively low latrine coverage.
A cluster-randomised study allocated 121 villages to intervention/control; baseline
was in 2011 and follow-up in 2013, roughly 18 months after completion. Households
with children under ten were enrolled; this was not an urban or nationwide sample.

**Published rationale; reconstructed short ToC:** participatory mapping, contamination
demonstrations, public commitments, committees and repeated follow-up → building
and using latrines/changing sanitation practices → less exposure → possible health
and growth gains. S1/S2 describe the rationale and intervention; this chain is
our reconstruction, not inspection of the original CLTS handbook or a verified
pre-intervention ToC document. No household hardware/financial subsidy was supplied;
that does not make delivery or household labour cost-free.

## Findings and limits

- **S1:** private-latrine access and reported sanitation behaviours improved under
  the tested package. Government open-defecation-free certification did not mean
  measured access/behaviour was complete or facilities were safely managed.
- **Primary diarrhoea outcome:** the trial did not detect a reduction at follow-up.
  This is not proof of equivalence or no possible effect in another season/context.
- **Secondary growth outcomes:** linear-growth measures improved, particularly for
  younger children, but overall weight-for-age and overall underweight findings
  were not statistically significant. Do not collapse different growth outcomes
  into a universal child-health benefit or claim that mediation was demonstrated.
- Follow-up was in the dry season; illness and behaviours were largely reported
  by carers, while anthropometry was measured. Missingness, baseline imbalance,
  multiple comparisons and untested biological pathways limit interpretation.
  Exploratory caregiver-attributed mortality findings are not a robust lives-saved
  estimate; all-cause child mortality did not differ.
- S1's Table 2 has an internal control-group latrine numerator inconsistency versus
  the abstract/narrative. This note does not silently recompute it or rely on that
  inconsistent count for a new estimate.

The intervention used shame/disgust as well as pride/dignity and public commitments.
S1 discusses concerns about poor households. Benefits on some outcomes do not settle
voluntariness, coercion, dignity, poverty or exclusion. These concerns are reasons
to question transfer, not tactics recommended by ItF.

Academic evaluators worked with Great Mali, whose data-collection leader was a
coauthor; implementation was by government/UNICEF. The Gates Foundation funded
the evaluation, with non-involvement and no-competing-interest statements reported.
Organisational separation does not establish complete reviewer independence.
S2 comes from the lead evaluator's institution and is not another independent trial.

## Pattern connections and local questions

**ItF interpretation:** C005 revision 1 informs [P002 revision 1](../patterns/keep-outcomes-distinct.md)
because latrine uptake, recalled diarrhoea, growth and certification did not all
move together. The trial tested a package, not a general claim that mobilisation
or local construction necessarily produces health gains.

For adaptation, examine practical household constraints, facility quality, water
and hygiene, maintenance, local norms and safe refusal. Do not assume everyone
can build/pay, prefer subsidy-free models by default, treat certification as
elimination, or transfer results to coercive implementation or dense urban settings.

Optional [L001/L002 questions, revision 1](../lenses.md): whose health, dignity and
labour count; who bears costs and can refuse; and what water/material/ecological
effects need examination? An original lens question does not validate this programme
or establish conformance with either framework.
