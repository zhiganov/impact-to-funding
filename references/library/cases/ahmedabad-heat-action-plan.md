# C001 — Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan

**Record revision 1; sources inspected 2026-11-08.** Domains: climate adaptation,
public health, municipal coordination. Historical case, not a current operating
status check or a transferable estimate of lives saved.

## Context and theory provenance

Following severe heat in Ahmedabad, India, municipal, public-health, research and
civil-society partners developed a heat action plan, piloted in 2013 and fully
implemented in 2014. The development account describes willing municipal leadership,
existing disaster-response capacity and trusted local partners as part of the setting.

**Source-published logic model:** S1 section 3.1 describes assumptions connecting
under-recognised heat risk, risk information, management strategies and health
capacity to lower heat risk and longer-term adaptation. The short pathway here
is an original paraphrase of that account, not a copied diagram or inspection of
the original plan PDF:

Local risk understanding and forecasts → warnings connected to public outreach,
preparedness and health-system response → possible reduction in harmful exposure
and health consequences. Effects depend on people and institutions being able to
act; mentioning an action in the model does not establish its delivery or coverage.

## Findings and limits

- **Design account, S1 sections 2.6 and 3.5:** warning levels were connected to
  interagency responsibilities, public communication and clinical capacity. A
  tabletop exercise exposed coordination issues and informed revisions. This is
  programme-reported implementation learning, not an isolated causal test.
- **Observational evaluation, S2:** the study compared April–June 2007–2010 with
  2014–2015, excluding development years. It reported a weaker post-plan relationship
  between heat and all-cause mortality and modelled avoided mortality. This is not
  a register of identified people saved or a randomised causal estimate.
- **Qualifying evidence, S2 Table 1 and Discussion:** post/pre warning-category
  mortality-rate comparisons were not statistically significant. Removing the
  exceptional 2010 season from the baseline left a declining trend but no significant
  differences. Do not headline only the more positive modelled result in Table 2.
- There was no contemporaneous untreated-city comparator. Secular changes, population
  estimates, cooling/housing access, pollution, seasonal timing and other factors
  limit attribution. Component effects, equitable reach, benefits after 2015 and
  transfer to another city were not established by these inspected sources.

The authors of both papers include programme participants and overlapping teams.
The evaluation reports NIH/CDKN support and funder non-involvement; conflict
declarations do not establish reviewer independence. This note does not label
the evaluation independent or reanalyse its mortality data.

## Pattern connections and local questions

**ItF interpretation:** C001 revision 1 informs [P001 revision 1](../patterns/response-behind-the-channel.md)
through the reported connection between warnings, responsibilities and response
capacity; no individual ingredient's mortality effect was isolated. It also informs
[P002 revision 1](../patterns/keep-outcomes-distinct.md) through the distinction
between preparedness, modelled associations and causal protection.

For transfer, examine local heat/humidity, lead time, authority, trusted relationships,
health capacity and residents' actual options. Advice to stop outdoor work is not
automatically usable by someone who cannot afford to do so. Do not copy thresholds
or effect sizes solely because another place also experiences heat.

Optional [L001/L002 questions, revision 1](../lenses.md): whose safety and livelihood
constraints shape the plan; who can act on a warning; and which ecological/resource
effects of protective measures need examination? These questions are not evidence
of full Doughnut or Planetary Boundaries conformance.
