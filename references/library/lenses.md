## L001 — Doughnut Economics

**Source:** DEAL [Principles & Guidelines](https://doughnuteconomics.org/principles-and-guidelines),
read 2026-11-08; edition context for the Doughnut itself remains in the dated
[framework notes](../frameworks.md#candidate-doughnut-economics). The principles
page is not an immutable edition of every diagram or underlying dataset.

**Purpose:** consider social needs and ecological limits together, including
distribution, participation, power, care and whether growth serves the work.
Retain both social foundation and ecological ceiling, rather than using only
the convenient half for a particular case.

Original questions that can be applied selectively:

- Whose needs are being served, who defines them and whose view is missing?
- Who can access the contribution, who bears the costs and who can influence it?
- What material, energy or ecological effects might extend beyond the immediate
  benefit? Which have actually been examined and which remain unknown?
- Does expanding the intervention improve these conditions, or should it change,
  remain small, be maintained or spread through others?

**Limits:** these questions do not establish a complete Doughnut assessment,
local agreement, ecological safety or an outcome. A positive social finding
does not prove ecological performance, and the absence of ecological evidence
in a case does not prove either harm or harmlessness. Differences in values
and accounts must not be replaced by an agent's synthetic consensus.

**Rights:** DEAL tools generally use CC BY-SA 4.0 with stated scope and exceptions;
stories, external sources and branding have distinct terms. No tools, diagrams,
stories or assets are imported here. Actual adaptations require their applicable
attribution/share-alike treatment, not relabelling as MIT. DEAL asks users not
to generate Doughnut diagrams with AI.

## L002 — Planetary Boundaries

**Source:** Stockholm Resilience Centre's
[Planetary Boundaries overview](https://www.stockholmresilience.org/research/planetary-boundaries.html),
as documented in the existing [reference checked 2026-11-05](../frameworks.md#candidate-planetary-boundaries).
This record reuses those dated notes; it is not a fresh scientific-literature,
dataset or displayed-graphic check.

**Purpose:** examine relevant environmental dependencies, interacting pressures
and possible burden shifting rather than treating a single local output as
evidence of overall ecological sustainability.

Original questions that can be applied selectively:

- Which environmental pressures could plausibly change through this activity,
  by what mechanism and compared with which alternative?
- Could reducing one pressure increase another, elsewhere or later?
- What system boundary, scale, period and evidence would support the specific
  environmental claim? What cannot be inferred from the available record?

**Limits:** this collection provides no method for assigning local or personal
shares of global boundaries. Do not invent quotas, use a domain label as proof
of ecological benefit, or treat one observed outcome as compliance with all
boundaries. An unmeasured environmental effect remains unassessed.

**Rights:** the existing notes report asset-specific CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 terms for
the overview's displayed 2026 graphics, not every source or dataset. None are
imported, redrawn, translated or adapted here.

## Applying a lens is an authored interpretation

Case and pattern links to L001/L002 revision 1 point to questions, not source-author
endorsement or empirical validation. Record which question matters locally and
what, if anything, it changes. Framework alignment does not establish impact,
eligibility for funding or permission to share private material.
