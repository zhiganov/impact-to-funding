# Case-grounded reference library

**Seed collection, record revision 1, prepared 2026-11-08.** Original source-linked
case notes and candidate patterns for exploring contributions and theories of
change. This is a small, deliberately selected collection, not comprehensive
coverage, a set of universal best practices or an independent impact assessment.
Historical evaluations do not establish that programmes still operate today.

The [design](../../docs/pattern-language-design.md) explains the distinction
between case evidence, pattern hypotheses, framework lenses and private local
applications. Record revisions below refer to these notes; source publication,
observation and retrieval dates are recorded separately in each case.

## How to read and use the collection

1. Start with the person's interests, situation or work. Domain names are useful
   discovery hints, not evidence that an intervention will transfer.
2. Read the relevant case's context, theory provenance, sources and findings.
   Distinguish the programme's own published explanation from this note author's
   reconstruction. Where a source is only summarised, do not imply its underlying
   study or dataset was independently reanalysed.
3. Treat pattern links as authored interpretations of particular observations or
   design features. A tested intervention bundle does not isolate every component
   or establish the effectiveness of the generalised pattern.
4. Use optional [framework questions](lenses.md) to expose needs, distribution,
   ecological pressures or omissions. Frameworks do not supply missing empirical
   evidence, local consent or a combined alignment score.
5. Examine differences in authority, incentives, capacity, relationships and scale
   before proposing a local adaptation. Keep expectations and open questions clear.

There is no requirement to create a personal theory, complete a template, make an
immediate decision or adopt a framework. Existing local knowledge may be more
useful than an external case. The [claim rules](../claims.md) still apply.

## Evidence and review limits

The notes separate reported observations, causal claims, programme explanations
and ItF interpretation. An evaluation's design, authorship, commissioning and
limitations matter; an external-looking publication is not automatically an
independent evaluation. Randomised, observational and qualitative findings are
not interchangeable. Absence of a measured benefit does not prove no possible
benefit; an intermediate outcome does not establish every downstream effect.

This is AI-assisted source curation, not practitioner validation or endorsement
by the projects, evaluators or framework authors. Candidate patterns remain
proposals even when several cases inform them. Counterevidence, missing outcomes
and unexamined transfer conditions remain visible rather than being averaged
into a confidence number or "successful project" badge.

## Privacy, rights and delivery boundary

Only original notes, links and attributed factual summaries belong here. Source
PDFs, diagrams, datasets, stories and long extracts are not bundled. Individual
notes record their source/rights inspection limits; the repository's MIT licence
does not relicense the sources. Follow [sources and rights](../../SOURCES.md).

Keep user conversations, contacts, affiliations, private project theories and
evidence outside this distributable collection. A local application is not a new
public case without a separate disclosure/reuse basis. Referencing a case does not
authorise outreach, publication, fundraising or assigning roles.

These are ordinary linked reference files, not a database, crawler, live search
service, available reviewer pool or automated recommendation system. No new
skill-routing or systems/complexity instruction changes are implemented by the
collection. Its use is optional, and it is not a gate for learning about ItF.
