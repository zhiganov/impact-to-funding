# Fictional theory reconstruction: Maple Desk

**Entirely fictional.** The project, records, dates, counts and participant
account below were invented together with the expected treatment. They are
authored examples, not model responses or a behavioural test result. No real
project or participant material was used.

Use the optional [reconstruction playbook](../playbooks/reconstruct-theory-of-change.md)
with the existing [learning](../references/learning.md) and
[claim](../references/claims.md) guidance.

## Request and bounded packet

> We run digital-help clinics. Here are our existing documents. Reconstruct how
> the project could help residents and identify where our explanation needs work.

The fictional observation period is June 2026. The supplied material ends at the
July planning note. No formal theory or funding application is supplied.

| Source | Supplied content |
| --- | --- |
| A1: project page v1, 1 June | "Workshops and our guide help residents complete council applications independently and avoid missed deadlines." |
| A2: delivery plan v1, 4 June | Two library clinics with computers and two mentors providing assisted form completion. A paid developer maintains the guide. There is no specified independent-practice exercise. Costs, mentor compensation and future staffing commitments are unknown. |
| A3: activity register v2, 28 June | Two clinics held, 12 attendance events, and eight application attempts involving seven residents: four marked submitted, three saved as drafts, one blocked by a council website error. Version 1 counted five submissions; v2 corrects one to a saved draft. Council acceptance, deadlines and unique people among the submitted attempts are not recorded. |
| A4: organiser notes v1, 28 June | A helper operated the device for two of the four submitted attempts. Assistance for the other two is unspecified. One participant account says: "We finished it together, but I still need help using that website alone." No other participant perspectives are supplied. |
| A5: guide change record v3, 18 June | The developer added a large-print version and corrected a broken link. The packet does not establish that clinic participants used this revision. |
| A6: next-period plan v2, 3 July | A proposal for continuing appointment-based assistance. It records neither a decision to drop the independence goal nor an agreed staffing or compensation arrangement. |

In a real assessment, such records would need an appropriate processing and
disclosure basis. The fictional packet supplies no authority to collect real
application details or contact residents.

## Expected first response

> The documents suggest two possible pathways: helping residents complete an
> application with assistance now, and helping them become able to do it
> independently later. The records support the first pathway more directly.
> Later independence and effects on deadlines remain unknown. Both purposes
> may matter, but the project page treats them as one.

This begins useful work before more intake. It gives a provisional explanation
and identifies a material distinction.

## Reconstructed views

### Stated theory: A1

Workshops and a guide → independent application completion → fewer missed
deadlines.

**Possible further benefit — analyst hypothesis:** timely applications might
improve access to council services. A1 does not state or demonstrate that link.

### Inferred working theories: A2, A4 and A6

- **Immediate assistance:** computers, mentors and shared form completion may
  reduce interface barriers during a clinic.
- **Learning through supported practice:** assistance could build later
  independence. The packet supplies no observation of that transition.

These can be complementary purposes. The mismatch does not establish that the
organisers misrepresented their intentions.

### Attributed experience: A4

One person reports shared completion and a continuing need for help. This
challenges a blanket independence claim. It does not represent all seven
residents or show that nobody learned.

### Supported observations and gaps: A3 and A5

The inspected register reports two clinics and the current attempt statuses.
Twelve attendance events are not twelve people. Four submitted attempts are not
necessarily four residents, accepted applications or avoided deadlines.

The guide record supports a bounded maintenance contribution. Its use and effect
during the clinics remain unknown. The developer cannot claim all application
outcomes; mentors, residents, library resources and the council system also matter.

## Focused trace: greater independence

| Connection | Proposed mechanism | Support and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinics/guide → opportunities to practise | A computer and guidance could make practice possible. | A2 describes assistance; A5 records a guide revision. Use of that revision and the amount of participant practice are unknown. |
| Practice → later independent completion | Participant-led practice might reduce the need for help. | No later unassisted attempt is supplied. A4 describes helper operation and one continuing need for help. |
| Independent completion → fewer missed deadlines | Less dependence on clinic availability might make timely action easier. | No deadline or comparison data is supplied. Existing help from elsewhere and other barriers remain unknown. |
| Timely application → access to a service | Submission may let the council consider a request. | Council decisions, eligibility, processing and actual benefit lie beyond this packet. |

The useful finding is the unsupported transition from assistance to later
independence. More workshop counts would not resolve it. The available records
also leave unknown what would have happened without the clinics.

## Compare and revise

- Use A3 v2's four recorded submitted attempts. Retain the earlier five as a
  corrected historical count, not another result.
- Describe current assisted completion separately from the independence
  aspiration. Proposed appointments do not prove the team abandoned learning.
- Keep the developer's guide changes distinct from participants' use and
  outcomes. Mentor compensation stays unknown.
- Offer a narrower account: "Maple Desk provides assisted access to online
  applications. Its June register records four submitted attempts; later
  independence and effects on deadlines remain unknown."
- A useful question for this fictional team is whether immediate assistance,
  independent capability or both define success. Its answer could change the
  work without requiring another survey.

Privacy of form data, mentor capacity and whether continuing assistance has
agreed staffing and compensation are plausible design concerns. These documents
do not establish that harm occurred. Perspectives of other attendees, people
unable to attend and service staff remain absent.

## What the example shows

The expected treatment starts from existing material, preserves more than one
explanation, traces a missing connection and revises a claim with its sources.
The existing [evidence worksheet](../assets/evidence-crosswalk.md) can retain
the relevant versions and gaps; no new record format is needed.

## Limits

The packet covers only its six fictional sources. Its report contents are not
independent corroboration of real events. The walkthrough supplies no project
agreement, impact evaluation or evidence of reliable host/model behaviour.
Private real-world working records belong outside this distributable package.
