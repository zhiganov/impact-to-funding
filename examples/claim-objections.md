# Fictional examples: looking for what counts against a claim

**Entirely fictional.** The projects, people, figures and sources below were invented together with the expected treatment. They are authored examples, not model responses or a behavioural test result.

Use with [claim review](../references/claims.md#before-a-claim-goes-public-look-for-what-counts-against-it).

## 1. The claim survives

**Claim for a public brief:** "Lumen Repair Café fixed 412 items in 2026."

- **Strongest objection:** the café's own log counts items brought in, not items fixed. A volunteer's note in the supplied material says some items were sent home unrepaired.
- **Checked:** the log has a separate "fixed" column. It totals 412. Items sent home are marked separately (97).
- **Who gains:** the café, which is applying for funding. The figure comes from its own log, so the brief calls it self-reported.
- **Decision:** keep. The brief says "fixed 412 items in 2026, by its own log".

## 2. The claim is narrowed

**Claim for a public profile:** "Ines's translation work made the Harbourside tenants' guide accessible to Spanish speakers."

- **Strongest objection:** a tenants' group survey, supplied by the person, says most Spanish-speaking tenants still asked staff to explain the guide.
- **Who gains:** Ines, whose profile it is. The tenants' group runs the survey and also seeks funding to translate further guides, so it gains from a gap being shown.
- **Decision:** narrow. The profile says "translated the Harbourside tenants' guide into Spanish". It doesn't claim the guide became accessible. The survey stays in the private assessment, and Ines decides whether to mention it publicly.

## 3. The objection's source has its own interest

**Claim for a report:** "Our open mapping tool is used by twelve local councils."

- **Strongest objection:** a competing company's blog post, found by a permitted web search on the report's date, says most councils "abandoned the tool within a year".
- **Who gains:** the project from its claim, and the competitor from its objection. Neither is independent.
- **Checked:** the project's own records show twelve councils signed up. Only five used the tool in the last three months.
- **Decision:** narrow and mark. The report says "twelve councils signed up; five used it in the last three months". The competitor's post is noted privately as an unverified objection from an interested source. The report doesn't repeat it as fact.
