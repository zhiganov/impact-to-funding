# Fictional walkthrough: a volunteer-library catalogue

**Entirely authored fiction:** no real library, participant, transcript or model
response is represented. These are constructed inputs and expected reasoning,
not observed facilitation behaviour, a validated method or an actual assessment.
Use with the [optional account guide](../docs/beneficiary-accounts.md).

## Starting report R1

An imaginary builder adds search to a volunteer library's catalogue. For an
illustrative four-week period, the builder proposes:

- **C1:** “The catalogue lets patrons find books without volunteer help.”
- **C2:** “The catalogue saves patrons time and reduces volunteer workload.”

The release establishes an output. No observation logs, timing measures,
representative sample or volunteer accounts are supplied. Neither benefit claim
is established by shipping the catalogue.

## Neutral conversation brief

> Understand experiences of searching for books during this four-week period
> to decide what, if anything, should change. The catalogue lists books and shelf
> locations. Ask about a concrete occasion of using, trying to use or not using
> it; what happened; what else mattered; and what the person would change.
> Do not assume benefit or ask the person to confirm C1 or C2. Explain actual
> audience/use arrangements and the option to skip or stop before questions.
> Offer a recap for correction without treating silence as confirmation.

No real invitation, consent arrangement, participant access path or service
configuration is established by this example.

## Illustrative report R2 and practical decision

> One supplied account describes finding a book without volunteer help alongside
> improved signage. Other accounts describe an outdated location and a mobile
> access barrier; the latter recap remains unconfirmed. These accounts do not
> establish how common the experiences are, time savings, reduced volunteer
> workload or a catalogue-caused improvement. C1 is narrowed to the described
> experience, with contrary accounts retained. C2 remains insufficiently
> supported. The next proposed action is to address location accuracy and mobile
> readability rather than expand the benefit claim. No owner or completion is
> established for that action.

This is private example wording, not permission to publish a real person's story.
Use the existing report's results/evidence, harms/burden and next-decision
sections. Retain R1 as superseded only if permitted; any R1 review remains bound
to R1, not transferred to R2. No actual review is supplied in this case.

If A later disallows export, stop that affected use and reassess the receiving
report against remaining permitted evidence; do not assume B and C are revoked
too. If C supplies a correction, update the account and controlled derivatives
within permission, keeping only authorised history. Neither event guarantees
recall of provider logs or prior exports. If nothing useful can be disclosed
safely, omit the story rather than demanding public evidence or hiding a
material limitation behind an unqualified benefit claim.
