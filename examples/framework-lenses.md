# Illustrative examples — optional framework lenses

**Entirely fictional; authored examples, not a behavioural test result.** People,
projects, sources and figures below are invented, not adapted from a private pilot.
No external project evidence was checked, no stakeholder was contacted, no outcome
was measured and no framework author reviewed or endorsed these examples. They
illustrate the [optional framework guidance](../references/frameworks.md), not an
official framework method or model-generated output.

## 1. A lens improves a question, not the impact claim

**Input:** Nia arranges a room for a shared repair project. An invented note E1
records two sessions; it contains no repair, participant-savings or emissions data.
The agreed project theory T1 hypothesises that access to repair support could help
people maintain possessions. Nia asks whether Doughnut Economics or Planetary
Boundaries would help decide a proposed change to appointment-only sessions.

**Authored response:** a social/ecological lens could help notice access burdens
and environmental assumptions. Who might be excluded by appointments? Whose actual
view is missing? Does repair displace a new purchase, and what materials, energy
or travel might change? These are questions, not findings. E1 does not answer them.
Use only those relevant to the current format decision; do not commission a full
framework assessment or survey merely to complete a checklist.

The pathway remains provisional beyond its actual support. Nia's room contribution
is not the sessions' whole causal effect. A possible change to T1 needs actual
project agreement; selecting a lens does not approve the change. Nia's motivations
can remain private, and others need not adopt a common personal purpose.

**Honest summary:** “The supplied note records two sessions. The framework lenses
raise access and resource-use questions for the proposed appointment format;
participant benefits, savings and avoided emissions remain unestablished.”
Neither alignment with a framework nor a new diagram would prove those outcomes.

## 2. Reject an invented global-to-person quota

**Input:** a fictional draft divides an unspecified global environmental threshold
by population, labels the result Nia's fair allowance, and claims that arranging
the room proves Nia stayed within all planetary boundaries.

**Authored response:** the calculation and claim are not established. No source
edition/control variable, defensible allocation method, unit, place/time, system
boundary, local data, uncertainties or ethical allocation basis was supplied.
Equal division is a value choice, not an automatic personal target. E1's session
count cannot fill those gaps or prove compliance with unrelated Earth-system
processes. Do not invent values or an environmental-compliance badge.

Keep any useful qualitative question about resource effects; omit the quota and
compliance claim. A later justified quantitative method would need its own actual
basis. No emissions calculation, comparative study or new software is required
to give that bounded answer.

## 3. Proceed without a framework or personal-purpose disclosure

**Input:** project member Ren has not written a personal theory and does not want
to discuss motivations. The project asks only whether an invented attendance note
supports “three sessions held.” Ren declines a named framework.

**Authored response:** proceed with ordinary claim review. The note lists three
scheduled sessions and identifies only two as held; the third status is unknown.
An honest formulation is “Two of three scheduled sessions were recorded as held;
the third status is unknown.” This is a delivery observation, not participant
benefit or Ren's exclusive causal contribution. No reviewer is supplied.

No framework selection, public profile, personal theory, withheld motivation or
collection of new personal data is needed for this project-only assessment. Do
not interpret Ren's choice as a lack of commitment or lower-quality evidence.

## 4. Keep source versions and shared credit separate

**Input:** Nia coauthored repair-access draft D1 with another project member. A
second project proposes reusing D1. A later source edition changes a concept
relevant to the assessment. A partner's existing review R1 examined only the first
project's count claim C1 revision 1.

**Authored response:** D1 remains one shared output, not two independent creations;
reuse does not prove either project's outcomes. Refer to the actual earlier
framework edition in the earlier assessment and label the proposed update. Do
not rewrite earlier project approval or personal intent. R1 does not review the
new edition, the reused draft, a revised theory or this portfolio. Repeated work
or funding costs must not be added again merely because two projects or lenses
refer to them. Unknown dates, costs, current review or source versions stay unknown.
