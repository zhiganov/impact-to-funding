# Fictional curation case: two guilds, one shared contribution

**Entirely fictional.** This extends the [Harbour story](guilds-itfti-atproto.md)
for the [#52 curation assessment](../docs/guild-curation-assessment.md). All people,
work, policies, roles, decisions, permissions and reviews are invented. No guild
is operating, manifest is published, account is created or user outcome observed.
The labels below are prose references, not JSON fields, schemas, real DIDs/CIDs
or deployable policy/selection records.

## Same work, different attributed selections

Sol's maintenance account v1 describes work on Lantern Tools and its limits. Ren's
guide v1 and Ivo's bounded guide-testing contribution are distinct shared-work
references. The tool predates Harbour; none of these references credits its entire
history or results to a guild. Existing support and the actual cost basis remain
unknown; no amount or second maintenance ask is invented.

For this case, Harbour's fictional policy P1 revision 1 recognises maintenance,
care and enabling work for learning in period A. Mira may curate the permitted
public selection; Mira and Ren must both approve a policy change under the guild's
invented rule. That curation mandate does not follow from a protocol role or make
Mira an independent reviewer. In the story, exact selection H1 revision 1 is separately
approved for the intended public audience with its attribution/disclosure limits.

Quiet Tools uses its own policy Q1 revision 1, focused on technical reuse. Ren
curates its selection Q1-selection revision 1 and discloses, where permitted,
being a collaborator rather than an independent evaluator. That policy and mandate
are separate from Harbour's; neither is an authority over Sol's own account.

| Fictional selection | Source/version and curator's meaning | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|
| Harbour H1 revision 1 under P1 revision 1 | Mira selects Sol's maintenance account v1 as enabling shared practice, alongside guide/care work where public use is cleared. | No verified accessibility benefit, exclusive guild credit or approval by every member. |
| Quiet Tools Q1-selection revision 1 under Q1 revision 1 | Ren selects that same maintenance account v1 for potential reuse, while noting that accessibility effects remain unknown. | No independent corroboration, new work, second outcome or additional maintenance cost. |
| Sol's optional individual view | Sol references maintenance account v1 with Sol's bounded role and may acknowledge only the corresponding specific statements. | Not agreement with either guild's whole policy, authority to speak for them or a complete public portfolio. |

The interpretations can differ without changing the source account. If Sol supplies
no reciprocal manifest, neither selection is thereby refuted. If both sides do
acknowledge the specific account, that is still not independent verification.
Neither guild owns the other publisher's canonical support representation.

Ivo is not a Harbour member; permitted recognition of Ivo's specific work does not
require joining or imply private-space access. Tavi is a current member with no
period-A contribution to report, not a person deemed inactive or in poor standing.
Ren declines public attribution for a separate newcomer-support activity without
a membership penalty. The public selection omits the protected activity/association,
not a row announcing a hidden contributor or a private reason. This narrator's
explanation is not content that the fictional public selection must disclose.

Any reference to an ATProto identity would distinguish the DID from a changeable
readable handle. This story creates neither, does not merge identities by name,
and does not require accounts for non-ATProto contribution. The current support
profile's local subject/report IDs are not substitutes for identity fields.

## A changed policy is not a revised history

Mira and Ren approve policy P2 revision 1 for a narrower selection of documented
reusable artifacts. The policy explains that this scope can leave care and other
less-visible work out; omission is not a finding that such work is unimportant or
did not occur. Neither policy chooses numerical weights, seniority rights or money.

Mira prepares new candidate H2 revision 1 under P2 revision 1, selecting guide v2
and maintenance account v1. H2 would replace H1 revision 1 only if actually approved;
the permitted historical H1 remains an account made under P1 revision 1. It is not
silently relabelled or made to appear retrospectively comprehensive under P2.
The new policy's approval does not approve H2's exact public wording or grant every
new use of contributor material. H2 remains a candidate pending its actual
publication/disclosure basis, rather than inheriting H1's approval.

The existing fictional peer checklist concerns guide v1 only, with its actual
collaborator/review limits. It does not review guide v2, Sol's maintenance account,
H1, H2 or either policy. A private-space record's MAC, a content CID, a public
signature or a group DID would not make those additional reviews exist. No source
or selection is called certified.

Ren raises a concern that the narrower policy under-recognises enabling work.
That is a policy disagreement, not an allegation that guide v2 is false or a
moderator's verdict on Ren. Mira and Ren can retain the concern and their actual
decision through the existing versioned collaboration process. The story invents
no staffed appeal body or promise of a response outside their stated roles.

## A permission change, a review withdrawal and a factual correction differ

Suppose permission for a limited source excerpt changes. Mira stops the affected
use while checking its actual audience/purpose/retention scope and reassesses
dependent wording against what remains permitted. That does not let Mira withdraw
the peer review on the reviewer's behalf or declare the underlying work false.
If required attribution cannot be supplied safely, Mira omits the reused material
or chooses another permitted presentation rather than falsely assigning authorship.

If the reviewer instead withdraws their own finding, it is not shown as current
endorsement; it is not silently replaced with Mira's statement. A separately
supported factual correction to a work account requires its own new version and
reference explanation. These actions may affect a controlled public view, but do
not promise to remove copies elsewhere or retain prohibited private history.

Sol's later departure can leave the permitted maintenance account identified as
historical work. It does not imply current membership, endorsement of P2 or a
retraction of all earlier contributions. A protected affiliation remains omitted;
no complete social graph, roster export or automatic identity matching is needed.

## How another tool could understand this today

A permitted prose account can preserve the curator, policy/version, selected
source/version, meaning, limitations and current correction/approval basis. An
appropriate person/collective `itf-manifest.json` can use existing subject,
description, report-reference and limitation meanings for support discovery;
it does not acquire typed policy/selection/contributor/DID fields from this story.
`derivedFrom` would apply only to an actual transformed support document under
its contract, not to the three sources a curator happens to mention.

A receiving tool must not infer machine-enforceable policy, inspected evidence,
current authority or an independent review from that prose. Missing or unsupported
typed meanings and restricted omissions remain explicit without disclosing private
items. Native authoring, Spaces reference compatibility and cross-client curation
behaviour have not been tested. Local readability is useful, but is not proof of
the intended native interoperability, collective approval or social benefit.
