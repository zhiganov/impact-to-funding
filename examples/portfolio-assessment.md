# Illustrative assessment — five-project contribution portfolio

**Authored example, entirely fictional; not a behavioural test result.** Based
only on the [synthetic input](portfolio-case.md). No external sources were checked,
no person was contacted and no funding was requested. Review and approval facts
below exist only inside the fictional scenario, not as real endorsements.

## Private decision and direction

Person A wants to strengthen practical cooperation and is exploring restoration.
These can remain two tentative personal pathways; there is no need to invent a
unified mission or five personal theories. The immediate decision is which future
commitments fit A's available time, not whether to claim five successful projects.

The projects retain their own shared theories. A's roles link to those pathways
without owning them. Other contributors' personal theories are unnecessary for
this assessment. Approval of Lantern and Seed plans is recorded in the fictional
sources; Bridge is a proposal, while Compass is unavailable and Harbour restricted.

## Capacity and attribution

Lantern's four allocated hours plus Bridge's proposed four would require eight
hours against six available in period B. This is a conflict between an existing
allocation and a proposal, not eight hours of accepted work. At most two hours
remain after Lantern, before any other commitments; other allocations are unknown.
Do not promise that two hours can deliver the four-hour adaptation.

The useful next decision is to decline, reduce or move the Bridge proposal if
A chooses, without changing another person's commitments. Unknown future work
on the other projects must not be filled with invented zero-hour allocations.

Reject "I created five successful community projects." The supplied material
supports a venue contribution, coauthored draft and inventory, alongside reuse
and noticeboard proposals. These are not five independent creations, exclusive
ownership or evidence that the projects achieved their intended benefits. No
combined impact score or percentage of causal credit is justified.

## Evidence, versions and review

R-L1 concerns only C-L1 revision 1 against E-L1 revision 1. It supports the narrow
period-A session-count wording within that fictional review's limits. It does
not review Lantern's revised theory T-L2, the new format's effects, A's causal
contribution or this portfolio. Independence remains unestablished.

R-L2 should not be called independent. Being outside Lantern is not enough: D
coauthored D1 with A, so D shares work and credit with the person whose claim was
checked. At most it was a partner check, with that relationship disclosed. D has
also withdrawn it, so it is no longer current support: mark it withdrawn and do
not cite it in a report or funding ask. The withdrawal does not show C-L2 is
false. E-L1 itself still records that A arranged the room, so C-L2 stays
supported within that note's scope, as supplied evidence without a current
review.

Retain T-L1 as the earlier intention, T-L2 as the changed plan, and the uncertainty
about access. A changed project pathway can prompt A to reconsider fit and venue
responsibility; it does not rewrite past work or imply A endorsed every revision.

## Funding and reporting views

F1 identifies one EUR 80 drafting cost with EUR 50 covered: EUR 30 remains, not
EUR 30 independently for each reuse. Bridge and Compass must not each request
that same remaining cost as separate new work. An agreed allocation could cover
it once. Actual later adaptations could have distinct costs, but none are supplied.
Other support, payment recipient, fundraising authority and future budget remain
unknown. This is cost analysis, not permission to coordinate or submit asks.

A project report could reference D1 as coauthored material and any documented
reuse; A's report could reference that same contribution without multiplying
creation or outcomes. Keep material limitations beside each claim. Neither view
inherits R-L1 or the project's funding authority by association, and neither
cites the withdrawn R-L2.

## Disclosure and next decision

Keep this assessment private. There is no approved public candidate. If one is
later requested, omit Harbour and protected motivations/associations without
implying the cleared subset is exhaustive; do not expose its source identifier
or suggest the omitted work was ineffective. Names, evidence links and exact
text/audience still require review. Private motivation is not required evidence
of project effectiveness.

For now, resolve the capacity choice before accepting Bridge's proposal and keep
claims limited to the supplied work. The review can be useful without a database,
public profile, complete personal mission, new stakeholder interview or more
evidence collection. It does not establish real-world usefulness or impact.
