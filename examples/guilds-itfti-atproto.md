# Fictional walkthrough: an Atmospheric Group as an ItFtI guild

**Entirely fictional.** Harbour Access Guild, its members, projects, funders, rules,
records and permissions are invented for the [concept note](../docs/guilds-itfti-atproto.md).
This is an authored design scenario, not a deployed group, schema, model response,
real invitation, funding offer, payment route or evidence of impact. No DID, account,
record, grant, participant dataset or network operation is created by this text.

## People working together, not just a funder-facing registry

The guild's shared practice is making small community activities more accessible.
Mira facilitates reflection and coordinates meeting access; Sol maintains a
document-conversion tool; Ren cares for guides and helps newcomers use them.
They work across two separate projects with different purposes and collaborators.
Their personal motivations need not be identical, disclosed or subordinated to
the guild. They can learn from each other even if no funding is available.

The fictional group has an agreed membership process, an elected technical
administrator and a separately agreed editorial process. Joining does not make
someone a project contributor, independent evaluator, spokesperson or payment signer.
The tool predates the guild; its earlier work is not credited to the guild retroactively.

## Bounded help, declined help and learning that changes a plan

This extends the [#53 workflow assessment](../docs/guild-workflow-assessment.md);
every action and decision below is invented, not a recorded interaction.

Mira asks for help simplifying one guide for the fictional Open Door project.
Ren offers one editing pass, not responsibility for every guide or future meeting.
Mira's request is not Ren's commitment until Ren accepts that exact scope. The
guild's shared interest does not require Ren to disclose a personal theory.

Sol declines a request to add a new export feature: Sol's available maintenance
capacity is already committed to a different project, Lantern Tools. Nothing is
assigned to Sol because a summary says the feature is useful. Mira narrows the
request instead of assuming unpaid work, replacing Sol or treating refusal as
poor standing. The project can defer the feature; no available alternative helper
is invented. Sol's private reasons need not be published.

In this scenario Mira and Ren are Open Door's agreed approvers, with both needed
for a changed working plan. They agree guide candidate v1; Ren separately accepts
only the editing pass. Sol's other project and its decisions remain independent.
Another fictional guild, Quiet Tools, also supports Lantern Tools: neither guild
owns that project or the whole result of Sol's contribution.

After the pass, Ren finds that some instructions assume a desktop interface. The
story supplies no participant experience or measured accessibility improvement.
Ren proposes testing the assumption before distributing the guide more widely;
Mira instead proposes a smaller revision that removes the desktop-only instruction.
No test or recruitment is launched by either proposal. Mira and Ren approve exact
candidate v2 as a limited interim plan, retaining Ren's unresolved concern that
other barriers remain unknown. An AI summary cannot call that unanimous confidence
or erase the objection. Their next learning question is whether the remaining
instructions work in the intended setting; owner, resources and consent for any
future check remain unestablished.

At their agreed reflection point, a short private account can say: one editing
pass completed, an assumption identified, a feature deferred and a plan revised.
It does not establish participant benefit or independent review. Mira's access
coordination and Ren's care for newcomers remain meaningful work even without
code commits, public testimonials or contribution scores. No additional guild
reporting cadence is required.

In a future native application, the request, Ren's accepted scope, Sol's declined
request and the versioned project decision are distinct attributed meanings,
not new record types selected by this example. Personal responses stay personally
authored; a guild-authored summary retains its acting editor and actual decision
basis. Neither a personal login nor a shared group session proves the permissions
or consent needed for these actions. Sensitive reasons and affiliations stay out
of any shared account. Where a safe supported carrier is unavailable, the step
is not claimed as a working native interaction.

## One group identity, different application responsibilities

In the proposed native design, the guild has one group DID and uses suitable
applications for its shared activities. The generic group spaces describe the
group and roles. ItF-specific contribution, reflection and decision content belongs
in an appropriate application/modality model, not an invented record collection
in this example or a universal group-metadata bucket.

| Fictional item | Author/decision basis | Intended audience and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Shared project account v1 | Project collaborators approve a bounded description; the guild does not own their whole theory or results. | Approved project/guild readers; a public excerpt would need a separate decision. |
| Sol's maintenance account v1 | Sol describes the work actually done, its period and limitations; others may add their own accounts. | Permitted guild reflection, not a claim of exclusive credit for the whole tool. |
| Mira's coordination account v1 | Mira describes access arrangements and unresolved concerns without identifying participants. | Permitted guild reflection; no attendance-to-wellbeing inference. |
| Ren's guide and newcomer support v1 | Ren and coauthors preserve shared authorship and correction history. | Recognised even when there is no code commit or promotional post. |
| Peer checklist v1 | A collaborator examines one guide version and records scope, conflicts and findings. | Partner/peer review, not independent impact validation or review of every related project. |
| Restricted account of an access barrier | A member supplies information for a narrow internal purpose only. | Not available to all members, funders or the public; its existence may also be restricted. |

Member-authored records would remain attributable to the members' repo hosts;
group-authored statements would be distinguishable. A third-party client may
assemble a permitted view, not infer collective approval from a group signature
or turn a MAC/CID into portable proof that the content is true.

The restricted item cannot be protected merely by hiding a row in a client that
has downloaded it. If no supported, suitable access boundary is available, it
stays outside the proposed shared workflow. Access to a group space also does not
authorise uploading its contents to an AI provider or funding-discovery query.

## A disagreement is not a moderator's impact verdict

A summary accidentally credits the tool's maintenance entirely to Mira, because
Mira presented it publicly. Sol challenges the attribution. Ren's documentation
and Mira's real coordination work remain visible; neither is dismissed as “just
communication.” The response corrects the specific shared-credit claim instead
of moving every contribution to Sol or awarding an AI-generated impact percentage.

The technical administrator can enforce the group's access rules. That power does
not settle the attribution dispute or let the administrator impersonate a project's
approval. The applicable policy version, permitted challenge, decision and remaining
disagreement are kept distinct from a content-moderation action. No private
allegation or sensitive association is published as part of making a correction.

If the guild later wants allocation weights, members must agree what those weights
mean and who decides them. This example chooses no method, numbers, treasury or
payment system. Compensation, causal impact and voting power are not interchangeable.
Historical recognition must not silently become permanent control over newcomers.

## Two support possibilities, no automatic hand-off of evidence

Fictional Funder A says it supports maintenance by informal collectives if an
acceptable fiscal arrangement is established. Funder B requests a public story
about an affected person. Neither is a real opportunity or a verified eligibility
match; no deadline, fee, identity document or application endpoint is invented.

The guild can use ItF to examine support needs and select a separately approved
aggregate account for A. A group DID and administrator role do not establish a
legal applicant, fiscal-host acceptance, representation mandate or payee. The
actual terms and these unresolved roles remain visible. The same maintenance
cost cannot be requested once for Sol and again as if it were new guild work.

B's request does not release the restricted account, even if the guild can read
other records. Its requirement stays unmet; a clarification or different permitted
presentation can be proposed without sending it. FtI can help interpret the supplied
funder terms without receiving the guild's private evidence or deciding who deserves support.

The outward account may use the preferred standalone `itf-manifest.json` convention
only if a separately curated support document is actually appropriate. The file
does not replace the private group workflow or prove eligibility. A funder's
`fti-manifest.json` would describe its funding intentions/opportunities, not grant
access to member records. No manifest is published in this scenario.

## Complementary public views, not complete directories

Suppose the guild prepares a collective `itf-manifest.json` presentation of its
selected shared work and support needs, while Sol prepares a separate individual
presentation of selected contributions across Harbour and another project. Either
view can stand alone; Mira need not publish an individual manifest, and the other
project need not publish a collective one. These are fictional prose descriptions
of possible views, not JSON fields or actual published files.

The guild's view recognises Sol's maintenance account v1 and Ren's guide v1 where
attribution is cleared. Sol's view references that same maintenance account v1,
describing Sol's bounded role rather than claiming the whole tool or every guild
outcome. If both publishers acknowledge that particular account, the reciprocal
references mean scoped acknowledgement, not independent review or agreement with
everything in the other view. The maintenance cost remains one cost, not two asks.

An invented non-member, Ivo, helped test the guide and permits that contribution
to be recognised without joining. A current member, Tavi, has no contribution to
report for this period; that does not imply poor standing. Sol can later retain
the historical maintenance account after leaving, clearly marked as historical
work rather than current membership or endorsement.

Neither view lists everyone's associations. Sol omits a sensitive affiliation;
the guild omits any attribution not cleared for that audience. Absence does not
mean no work or relationship exists. Where the native design references an
ATProto identity, it would use the DID with a readable handle that may change;
this example creates no identity or handle. Non-ATProto participation remains valid.

The technical group roster/access records, contribution claims or recognition,
and these separately curated public views remain distinct. No roster is exported
automatically. A group administrator's access cannot supply publishing consent,
representation authority or applicant eligibility. The current support contract
defines no contributor/guild relationship arrays or DID fields; any typed addition
requires a separate decision, not an inferred extension from this example.

## Departure, correction and continuity

Sol leaves the guild while remaining involved in one project. Current membership
changes; the permitted historical account is not rewritten to erase the work or
treated as a continuing endorsement. Access and any shared sessions must be
reassessed under actual host and application rules, not an assumption that roster
acceptance alone controls access.

A future host handover would need to account separately for member-authored repos,
group-authored repos, policy and claim versions, restricted copies and host-private
state. Migrating a group repository does not promise to migrate moderation queues,
join requests or OAuth sessions, nor to erase third-party copies.

The people may keep working together through permitted local documents while
native readiness is unresolved. That preserves useful collaboration but does not
demonstrate the intended cross-application ATProto experience. The existing host/
client readiness and synthetic pilot gates still apply before a live experiment.
