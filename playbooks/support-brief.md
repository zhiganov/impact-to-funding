# Prepare a support brief

Use this playbook when a support or funding brief would help, or when the person
asks for one. A brief can explain a financial need or other support, such as time,
access or shared resources. It is optional for exploring direction, nonfinancial
support and paid work. The root [skill's boundaries](../SKILL.md#boundaries-that-always-apply)
and [privacy guidance](../PRIVACY.md) always apply.

Start with the person's context and authorised material. Use the considerations
below where they help, rather than as a fixed sequence or an intake form. Ask a
focused question only when it would materially help. A brief can be provisional
when the budget, recipient, evidence or funder is unknown. A named or completed
theory of change is optional, including for a support brief. Ordinary exploration
needs no legal identity.

## Shape the brief

- **Purpose and costs:** Describe the work, the person's actual or proposed role,
  and what additional support would enable. Explain the rationale in ordinary
  language if useful. Give known amounts, currency, period and cost basis; otherwise
  say what is unknown. Include capacity, dependencies and learning or progress
  plans where they matter. Do not invent commitments or deadlines.
- **Recipient and authority:** Identify the intended recipient and their actual
  authority where known. A fiscal host may be relevant to a particular opportunity;
  use facts and terms for that opportunity rather than assuming a host or its fees
  apply elsewhere. Leave unresolved host, authority and eligibility questions open.
- **Existing support and overlap:** Compare known support, commitments, cost items
  and periods across relevant asks. Do not charge the same cost twice. A genuine,
  distinct adaptation cost may qualify separately if supported and consistent
  with the actual terms. Preserve any specific overlap or disclosure restrictions;
  unknown support is not zero. See [contribution guidance](../references/contributions.md).
- **Claims and evidence:** Separate supplied accounts from sources actually
  inspected, and describe the extent and dates of inspection where useful. Keep
  claims within their evidence and preserve other contributors, contrary findings
  and material uncertainty. Use material only when processing with this host is
  permitted for the purpose. Restricted evidence may inform a private draft under
  those permissions. Check permission to state a claim separately from permission
  to cite or disclose its evidence for the intended use and audience. A supported
  claim authorised for that audience can be stated without exposing restricted
  evidence. Explain access limits only where safe. If support is missing, narrow or
  omit the claim, or mark it provisional; retain uncertainty. If permission to
  state it is missing, use authorised wording or omit it for that audience. See
  [claim review](../references/claims.md) and
  [evidence and reporting](../docs/evidence-reporting-design.md).
- **Possible funders:** Research possible funders only when requested or otherwise
  within the authorised task, and the host permits it. Keep research read-only.
  Inspect relevant primary sources and record source and inspection dates, the
  actual opportunity and round, and any known terms or gaps. Check whether a round
  is current rather than assuming it is open. When requested, produce a short list
  of relevant leads with known terms, round status and uncertainty. The list records
  possibilities; it provides no introductions. Check offers and eligibility separately.
- **Funder rules on AI assistance:** Before drafting prose for a named funder,
  check what the funder says about AI-assisted applications. Use the funder's own
  form, guidance or terms, and note where and when you found the rule. Some funders
  reject applications written with AI. If AI writing is not allowed, prepare a
  private fact sheet the person writes from instead: facts, figures, evidence, the
  funder's exact questions and any word limits. Offer proofreading only if the
  funder permits it. If you can't find a rule, say so and let the person decide.
  See [funder rules on AI assistance](../references/funding-and-review.md#funder-rules-on-ai-assistance).

## Present the result

Use short prose suited to the person's purpose and intended audience. Distinguish
observations, proposals and unknowns. Explain the support sought, its known cost
basis and what it could enable. Include relevant limits and any useful learning or
review plan. A final brief should keep unresolved facts visible too. Use the
[funding and disclosure guidance](../references/funding-and-review.md) for the
appropriate audience and approval boundaries.

If useful information is missing, offer a clear provisional draft or a short
outline. The person can correct it without first completing a questionnaire,
register or evidence packet. Keep a working draft private by default; write a
file only when requested and in an appropriate authorised location.

## Optional formats

Prepare a `funding.json` candidate only if requested and appropriate to real OSS
work and supplied financial facts. A general support profile is a separate,
curated candidate: use `itf-manifest.json` if desired, including for nonfinancial
support. Follow the [support-profile guide](../docs/support-profile-tools.md).
Leave missing facts open rather than inventing an account or funding destination.

## Limits

Eligibility, legal or tax status, recipient authority, impact and funding received
each depend on their own evidence. Leave them unknown when unconfirmed. Do not score
people or causes. Decline assistance that would materially enable serious harm, and
follow the root skill's responsible-assistance boundary. Do not imply another
person's consent or approval. Publication, contact, applications, account changes
and payments each need their own applicable authority. Matching services and
guaranteed support are outside this skill.
