# Reconstruct a theory of change from existing work

Use this optional playbook when someone wants to understand how existing work
could lead to change, infer an implicit pathway, or compare stated intentions
with practice. Start from their material and question. Read the
[learning](../references/learning.md) and [claim](../references/claims.md)
guidance for mechanisms and evidence.

## Begin with a useful explanation

Use permitted descriptions, plans, budgets, activity records, reports, decisions
or accounts from people involved. A short conversational account can be enough
for a provisional explanation. A formal theory, complete packet, personal
mission or funding goal is optional.

Describe the subject and period where known. Say which material you inspected,
what it covers and what remains unavailable. Ask only questions that materially
affect this work; a missing field need not stop a bounded answer.

Begin with a short explanation of how the work could help and its strongest
uncertainty. Choose only the operations below that serve the question. They are
ways to continue, not commands, stages everyone must complete or new tool APIs.

## Reconstruct an overview

Keep these views distinct:

| View | What belongs here |
| --- | --- |
| Stated theory | What the project explicitly says it seeks and how. Attribute it to the source and version. |
| Inferred working theory | An analyst's explanation suggested by activities, resources and arrangements. Keep competing explanations visible. |
| Attributed experience | What people actually report, with their role, scope and source limits. Leave absent perspectives unknown. |
| Supported observations and gaps | What inspected material supports, contradicts or leaves open. Reading a report establishes its contents, not independent confirmation of its claims. |

Identify relevant actors, resources, activities, outputs and possible outcomes.
Explain proposed mechanisms, assumptions, authority, capacity and dependencies.
Keep individual contributions separate from shared results. Include other
contributors, possible harms, delays and feedback when they matter.

Several pathways may coexist. Preserve a missing or incoherent explanation
instead of completing it with plausible prose. Outcomes, intentions and
mechanisms can each remain uncertain.

## Trace one outcome

Follow a change of interest backwards through the contributions and conditions
it would require. For each useful connection, identify:

- the proposed mechanism and who could act;
- supporting sources or why it remains a hypothesis;
- conditions outside the project's control and other contributors;
- contrary evidence and plausible alternative explanations;
- what would weaken or change the explanation.

Use the claim guidance to assess support within its actual scope. A table,
chain or prose may suffice; a complete causal graph is unnecessary. Keep branches
and feedback where supported, and stop at unsupported connections.

Attendance, delivery and usage can support their own claims. Give a later
outcome its own basis rather than treating another activity count as the
missing link. Ask what could happen without this contribution, while leaving
an unmeasured counterfactual unknown.

## Compare explanations or versions

Compare an earlier promise with later plans, resource choices, delivery and
available accounts. Preserve dates, definitions, exact source versions and
corrections. A revised count replaces the old count for that period; it does not
create another result or inherit an earlier review.

Distinguish a changed record from a changed activity or goal. A mismatch may
reflect adaptation, supported practice or competing purposes. Compare those
explanations instead of inferring hidden motives or treating one account as
everyone's experience.

## Reframe the theory or the work

Offer a bounded revision that responsible people can accept, challenge or leave
open. It might separate immediate assistance from later capability, narrow a
claim, change an activity, support existing work, do less or stop.

Explain what the revision preserves and what remains unknown. A useful question
about purpose may matter more than another survey. Propose evidence collection
only when it could change useful work and its burden is justified; apply the
learning guidance's authority, capacity and stopping conditions.

Reuse existing claim records and, when helpful, the
[private evidence worksheet](../assets/evidence-crosswalk.md). A concise note
with optional local references can suffice. Keep one source of claims rather
than another ledger.

## Leave a useful result

Prefer an explanation suited to the person: a provisional pathway, source-linked
connections, the strongest contradiction or gap, and a useful interpretation or
open question. A conversation may be enough. Keep saved working material outside
this distributable package and prepare audience-specific views separately.

The [Maple Desk walkthrough](../examples/theory-reconstruction.md) illustrates
this approach with an entirely fictional packet.

## Limits

Apply the skill's shared permission, privacy and source-as-data rules. Use only
material permitted for the actual host and provider. Further collection, contact
and publication need their own authority.

Reconstruction produces revisable explanations. It cannot recover true motives,
establish causal impact or supply affected people's agreement. This playbook
adds source guidance, not a collector or evaluation service. Its fictional
example describes expected treatment, not observed model behaviour.
